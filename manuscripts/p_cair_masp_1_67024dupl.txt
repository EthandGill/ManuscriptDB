[TRANSLATION]
1     Having come to us he informed us that he hails from this
2     village of the Thebaid, and declaring that his own father,
3     who was a property-owner in it, having become a leading man,
4     and collecting the contribution-taxes on behalf of the whole estate,
5     used to deposit these to the officials of the local order; but since
6     they suffered no ordinary injustices at the hands of the magistrates of the day,
7     they gave themselves over to our imperial house and came under its protection, but Theodosios, the most magnificent,
8     seizing on the absence of the father of the petitioner, collected the taxes of the village,
9     but deposited nothing at all
10    to the public account, so that the officials of the local order
11    again, a second time, exacted from the suppliants the contribution-taxes
12    laid upon them; and concerning this they have already
13    procured divine letters of ours written to your gloriousness,
14    but that man's intrigue prevailed more than our
15    commands, so that for the petitioner the matter has come to a second occasion
16    of journeying and to greater delay. We decree
17    therefore that your gloriousness now at least, by the divine letters given
18    concerning this to the suppliant, put the fitting end to the
19    matter, and that he, or rather the
20    village belonging to him, not be deprived from time to time of what is owed to them, so that, on this pretext, no weakness
21    may a little later arise for them concerning the payment
22    of the public taxes. And since he says that certain of those
23    who own property in this village have taken away, contrary to the principle of justice,
24    the goods of the petitioner and of his
25    brothers; we decree your gloriousness,
26    examining the matters concerning these, if you find them so,
27    to make provision that satisfaction be done according to the law to the petitioner and his brothers.
28    In addition to these they informed us
29    that Ioulianos, pagarch of the Antaiopolite, wished to bring their
30    village under his own pagarchy,
31    and that too although they had never paid under any pagarchy of theirs, but according to the manner of the
32    self-collecting (autopract), depositing the public taxes through themselves to
33    the local order. But since they did not endure
34    that he should fall upon them in this regard and commit
35    a seizure of property, and that so great an outrage of his should simply arise
36    against them and their village, so as even to bring it under the pagarchy, a thing beyond
37    all outrage, we decree therefore your gloriousness
38    to investigate the matters concerning this with all the precision the law commands,
39    and if in truth you find that those dwelling in this same village have never
40    paid under a pagarchy, to remove the aforesaid man
41    from his association with them, and to make provision to remedy for the petitioners the damages inflicted by him
42    upon them, as seems good to the laws concerning this. And your gloriousness
43    shall also bring the others, as many as may appear, both of your staff and of the civilians, liable to the petitioners both in
44    monies and in charges;
45    and the matters that look to monies you shall remedy for the
46    petitioners according to the law, and concerning the charges
47    you shall take care to subject to lawful penalties those who have
48    committed these things, so that satisfaction be done in each respect, both to the petitioners
49    and to the law. Of the things procured by collusion, as is likely, taking together
50    contrary to what has now been decreed by us, we decree that they can have no
51    force; you shall make provision for the matters that look to monies, and concerning the charges you shall subject those who have offended to lawful penalties, so that satisfaction be done in each respect, both to the petitioner and to the law, these things …

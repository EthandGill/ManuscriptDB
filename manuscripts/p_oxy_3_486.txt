[TRANSLATION]
1   To Iulius Varianus, epistrategos of the Seven Nomes and of the Arsinoite,
2   from Dionysia daughter of Chairemon, her mother being Hermione daughter of Chairemon, of those
3   from the metropolis of the Oxyrhynchite nome. A dispute having been brought against me
4   by a certain Sarapion son of Mnesitheos, who, regarding a vineyard property and grain-
5   lands which I bought from his father back in the 11th year of Hadrian Caesar the lord, having paid
6   to the father himself and to a certain creditor of his the agreed-upon price
7   and having received the proper public registration of the purchase, alleged that I held
8   them in trust, Claudius Quintianus, the former epistrategos, having heard (the case), re-
9   ferred it to the most excellent prefect. And I from then on attend constantly at the
10   prefect's tribunal, but since my opponent has been negligent and has not appeared,
11   I submitted to the most excellent prefect a petition, a copy of which I have ap-
12   pended, setting forth the disposition of the ... matter, and he sent me on to you
13   the lord to have the case judged. Since then the opponent is not present even now, and the
14   sowing-season presses and the care of the (lands) swept away by the river
15   requires my presence, I request, if it seem good to you, that you permit me to sail up to be
16   judged by you on the spot, that I may be benefited. Farewell.
17   And the following is the copy of the petition which I submitted to the most excellent prefect:
18   To Titus Flavius Titianus the most excellent prefect,
19   from Dionysia daughter of Chairemon, her mother being Hermione, of those from the city of the Oxyrhynchites.
20   A certain Sarapion son of Mnesitheos from the same city, before Claudius Quintianus the
21   former epistrategos of the Seven Nomes, accusing my mother Hermione of poison-
22   ing and concerning certain possessions, made up a story that there were being withheld
23   from him (the properties) which I, Dionysia, bought according to public registrations, having
24   paid the price for them to his father while living and to creditors of the same
25   father, with whom the said properties were held under mortgage,
26   asserting that they had been registered in trust, and the whole matter having been
27   referred by the epistrategos to you the benefactor, it happened that my moth-
28   er died before the trial, and I from then on, by the
29   epistrategos's letter, having been ordered along with Sarapion to sail down
30   here, arrived, Sarapion not having troubled himself to sail
31   down. Since then, while I am staying here, it has been reported to me that all my property
32   from the excessively high rise of the most sacred Nile has been destroyed,
33   both farmsteads and lands and embankments, I beseech you, lord prefect, the
34   opponent not being present even now, to permit me to sail up so that my own
35   rights I may obtain, lest together with my property I too perish of famine,
36   [that I may be benefited]. Farewell. Year 16 of Hadrian Caesar, Phaophi 12.
37   [...] has (it); petition the epistrategos. Deliver it.

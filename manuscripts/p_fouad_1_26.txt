[TRANSLATION]
1     To Marcus Sempronius Liberalis
2     prefect of Egypt
3     from Pharion son of Eutyches son of Herakleides, of those from the metropolis
4     of the Arsinoite nome. The petition which I submitted to you
5     the lord, and under which I obtained
6     a subscription, by which I was referred under the number
7     233 to Severus, the one in charge of the adjudicated cases, it was said to us … a copy I have appended.
8     Since then … of the
9     my adversary Heron son of Amatios, former exegete of the city of the Arsinoites,
10    by his insolence and force being very powerful in
11    the locality, and I shall not be able against
12    him before such judges, since he is a very
13    great magnate. So that you may know the insolence of Heron:
14    a certain Paulinus, son of a gymnasiarch, appealed to Eudoros, the strategos of the Themistos
15    and Polemon (divisions), bringing a charge against
16    Heron, and the [strate]gos summoned
17    Heron and he behaved insolently, and
18    the strategos concerning him
19    recorded a memorandum. Lord governor, I have shown you
20    the insolence of (my) adversary; I beg,
21    lord, savior of Egypt, hear me against him, that I may be able to
22    remain in my own (place). And there is a copy
23    of the petition. To Marcus Sempronius
24    prefect of Egypt, from Pharion
25    son of Eutyches son of Herakleides. Since your innate benefaction, lord governor,
26    has reached all things, and (since) I myself,
27    being in many ways oppressed and wronged, hastened to take refuge with you to obtain justice. And the
28    matter I set out: when need came upon me, I borrowed from Heron son of Amatios, former exegete of the city of the Arsinoites,
29    a little silver at various
30    times; so that, trusting in the dignity of his office, being very powerful in the locality,
31    he continually brings insults and injustices upon me, although informed of the (interest) which by force he compelled me
32    to promise, stater-rate interest,
33    which exceeded the principal almost even by half as much again. On account of which I am in danger of having to abandon (my home) and emigrate. Whence, taking refuge with you the savior of all, I also ask, if it seems good to your fortune, to hear me against him, so that
34    the (sums) which he received from me may be reckoned up
35    and if it appears from the account of reckoning
36    justly owed to him he may receive (them)
37    so that I may be able, through your benefaction, to obtain (justice).

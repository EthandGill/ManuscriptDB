[TRANSLATION]
1   To his sister, Hermias greeting.
2   For the rest, what to write you I do not know, for I have worn my-
3   self out telling you each thing, and
4   you do not listen. For a man seeing
5   himself in misfortune ought even to with-
6   draw, and not simply to fight
7   what is fated. For though we have a birth of moderate
8   and unlucky lot, not even
9   so do we attend to ourselves. So far, then,
10   nothing at all has yet been done;
11   still, even so, if you care, send
12   me someone — either Gunthos or Ammonios —
13   to stay with me until I know
14   how my affairs are settled. Am I being
15   dragged along, or even shut out, until
16   God takes pity on us? For Hermei[as]
17   is in haste to come to you, but, though I
18   asked him to stay, he was not willing,
19   saying that he has some necessary business
20   and must go up; and my son Genna-
21   dios is not able to attend to the
22   property, especially since he is in a foreign place and
23   with the (military) corps. Manage your own affairs
24   as is fitting, lest we be utterly over-
25   turned. For we have not resolved to have
26   anything (more), being in misfortune. Farewell to me, ever
27   faring well.
28   Hermeias to Gunthos his son, gree(ting).
29   If Ammonios does not come at once
30   to me, you yourself,
31   putting all aside,
32   come in his stead, doing your own work.
33   But see that you do not
34   leave me afflicted.
35   And let me know how
36   Didymos fares; do the
37   days bring all things to completion?
38   I pray for your health.

[TRANSLATION]
1     … brother, the troubles and the headaches of this city, apart from …
2     … and the weeping of those put aboard the boat, and this …
3     … from the time that Baroch the most glorious came here, and the lord John the notary, until this hour …
4     … to pray; for I have detained the lord Origenes here, thinking that he would be released …
5     … to be found, and I shall pay my respects to you. To which end I wrote to you even before the matter, and to whatever …
6     … of the balance owed by us, I barely found a little relief; so then, since I have received certain …
7     … and there is need that I pay my respects to my lords and meet with them; and at the present …
8     … to be brought up to the city and to be greeted by our common sister and the children …
9     … so that I may attain what has been striven for; but write ahead to me by your groom, so that, God …
10    [commanding] … I may travel about the alarm and meet you. For through God the public taxes …
11    … of the presbyter, by your zeal, as you wrote. For concerning the lord Origenes …
12    … I report to you; if you command anything, I do it; for I compelled him to stay until I receive …
13    … ; for it turned out to my trouble and not to relief. But yes, my lord, how many things …
14    … this small relief, so that we may greet one another. For the same does …
15    [To my God-protected] lord and brother Papas, the greatest comes, member of the council [and pagarch, from Pesynthios].

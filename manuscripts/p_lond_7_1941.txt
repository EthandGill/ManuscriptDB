[TRANSLATION]
1     Hierokles to Zenon, greeting. If you are well and in other respects are getting on [according to your mind],
2     it would be good. I myself am also in health. You wrote to me about Pyrrhos, that if [we] kn[ew]
3     for certain that he will win, (we should) train him, but if [not], that it should not happen that he be [torn away] from his stud[ies]
4     and that vain expense be incurred. From his studies then he has n[ot been at all torn away],
5     but he is making progress, both as to the rest of his lessons. As for knowing for certain, the gods [most of all] would know, but of those now existing Ptolemaio[s] says that he will far surpass (them), [although for the present he falls short]
6     in that they had a long time's start, whereas we are just [beginning to train him. And]
7     know that Ptolemaios does not exact wages like the other train[ers, but he simply hopes that you]
8     will crown him, in return for which, although he was a stranger to him, you earlier wished to do good and …
9     you are doing about the palaestra. Take thought also about the mattress about which [I wrote to you before]
10    so that you may bring it down. And buy a chest for six drachmas and bring it down. And send also [two jars of honey],
11    so that we may have it; for it is useful.
12    Hierokles to Zenon, about the boy.
13    Year 29, Xandikos 2,
14    in Memphis.

[TRANSLATION]
1     ☧ (Chi-Mu-Gamma, Christian invocation)
2     The divine providence and our Christ-loving emperor granted your most excellent benevolence as a gift to the all-wretched
3     land of the Thebans; he deemed it worthy that it should govern, and as
4     being sufficient to suppress the bitter wrongs of the Thebans; and having learned this
5     precisely, at the very foot of your glorious tracks
6     we have come rolling, lamenting, explaining the matter concerning us
7     as it stands in these things. For at our first entry, before the fearsome
8     glorious tribunal of yours, having availed ourselves of it, being afraid
9     of your loftiness, we were not able to declare the calamity
10    … against Florentius, the devoted soldier, the agent,
11    and the unspeakable evils that he has done to us from him, and
12    to our defenceless and widowed all-wretched city of Antaios.
13    For he silenced us … and our …
14    he shut off our argument, just like the filth he made by night, those
15    … speechless of the sun; therefore by these humble
16    and revered letters, again reporting,
17    we have made use, wishing that your supernatural benevolence
18    should know everything concerning us. We therefore teach
19    your supernatural authority, that in the prelude of
20    his sojourn into our city, while he was vicar
21    of those soldiering in the city, the soldier of the regiment of the most noble Scythians,
22    together with … these wicked Saracens …
23    … of your glorious sojourn, inasmuch as …
24    … so as to suppress every injustice and to overturn every
25    wicked thing, we rejoiced like those who were then in Hades
26    awaiting the coming of Christ, and we waited to be shown mercy,
27    since … now … the …
28    … the … second and honoured …
29    … the … indeed …
30    … in the body …
31    … tracks … robber … from which to impose
32    they hold up of the daily portions, and the surcharges of the public treasury beyond the privilege,
33    and the creditor himself Florentius and those like him prevailed to master all their own things on account of the …
34    … of the gold … of wheat 30 artabas and of barley 60 artabas and of wine 100 jars [and of vegetable 12 artabas] … other …
35    by the public measure … and to the wretched landholders, and for scarcely 3 solidi alone
36    they spend all his produce; from this … surcharges into
37    public dues and … the patron creditor … answers for the … so great …
38    … for he stood by measure … and the land was utterly overturned, and this
39    we teach your glorious benevolence, that when the wicked
40    barbarian Blemmyes, in the time of our forefathers of old, having seized and taken
41    [our city] and ravaged it grievously, no longer from those times
42    has the wretched city and its tax-collectorship … of it
43    … … …
44    For from then both our imperial and public baths … the greatest
45    were there until the present, but now there is a private bath in the city,
46    serving the poor who bathe for some coppers; and from the time
47    when the said Florentius entered the city, bending down … when bathing …
48    … with … indeed … toward him
49    we too went away wishing to bathe … from … one
50    of a festal solemn holy day, and this man did not hesitate to come
51    … and to plunge our garments into the sink
52    or to dip them, and to disgrace us as we walked to our own homes.

[TRANSLATION]
1     Marcus to Antonia and Sarapion and Cassianus,
2     my parents, very many greetings.
3     I make your obeisance before the gods who share the temple;
4     for no one is able to go up to make obeisance because of the
5     battle that has taken place of the men of the upper (districts) against the soldiers. There have died
6     15 soldiers of the singulares, apart from the legionaries
7     and the reservists (evocati) and the wounded [and] the disbanded (?).
8     Now therefore I write to you that quickly, whatever is being made, send me
9     the woolen tunic, that I may find it (ready) on me. And let the palm-grove
10    be watered thrice, as at such a season, and let Cassianus sell his arouras
11    again, as many as are there, and let him settle up all
12    our outstanding affairs; let him take a receipt as a discharge from
13    the Theban. And I wish you to know [th]at I have not been able to go
14    to the parts of Cassianus; I do not know whether the mother of his children has gone out
15    or not; for when Ca[s]sianus was setting out he charged me to write to him about the affairs of the city of Kynon (Cynopolis) and whether the bread is being seized.
16    And as in each letter I write to you to shake out my medical
17    books, shake (them) out and take them from the window, which I left
18    when I was setting out. And Apollonios the blind man came to me
19    sa[ying to me th]at Serapiakos has released to your mother the arouras.
20    If [then] he has released (them) and you know the mind of this Apollonios the blind man, that he is fair-minded, l[e]ase them to him,
21    if that other man is not for you, giving in full the grain.
22    Greet Eudokeia the wife of Octaeus with the
23    children, and Valerius the son of Pasikas, and Arse[nion and] all, and the mistress and my sister
24    … greet all [by n]ame.
25    Greet also Gaion and Valerius the pitch-dealer. I pray for your health.
26    Deliver to Sarapion and Cassianus, the fathers.

[TRANSLATION]
1     Year 51, Payni 8, in Diospolis the Great of the Thebaid, before Ptolemaios, one of the Friends and
2     hipparch over men and epistates of the Perithebas (district), there being present Ptolemaios, son of Agatharchos,
3     one of the Friends and hipparch over men; Eirenaios, son of Eirenaios, of the same; Polyperchon,
4     son of Ammonios, of the katoikoi cavalry; Sesoosis, centurion of the eager king-loving (guards); and others.
5     Hermias, son of Ptolemaios, of those from the Ombite (nome), having entered suit against Horos and those with him,
6     the choachytai of those from Diospolis, there was read the memorandum submitted by Hermias,
7     of which this is a copy:
8     To Ptolemaios, one of the Friends and hipparch over men and epistates of the Perithebas,
9     from Hermias, son of Ptolemaios, a Persian, of the successors about the court and commander over men.
10    Having been wronged not moderately and being in danger of being deprived of my own property by Horos, son of [Horos, and Chonnonpreus, son of]
11    Harsiesis, Psenchonsis, son of Teephibis, Panas, son of Pechytis, choachytai, and the others,
12    for whereas there belong to me in Diospolis ancestral houses, of which both my forefathers
13    had ownership as long a time as they survived, the accused, having their dwelling
14    in the Memnoneia, and in the disturbance of the times having decided that (I) had
15    my dwelling elsewhere, having come upon a certain one of the aforementioned houses, which is
16    on the south and west of Diospolis, north of the avenue leading to
17    the river of the greatest goddess Hera, whose walls had been built, and using
18    the force at their disposal, having repaired the demolished parts, they dwell there
19    laying claim to it unjustly. Concerning which, having learned of it in the 46th year, having come
20    to Diospolis, I submitted against them a memorandum to Hermias, one of those of equal rank with the
21    kinsmen and strategos and nomarch; and though they were ordered to come to the
22    council-chamber, being conscious to themselves that they have nothing sure, they evaded. And likewise also
23    in the 49th year, in Mesore, when Hermias came to Diospolis and I petitioned about the same matters
24    and they removed themselves, he instructed Hermogenes, the then
25    chief of police, to hand over to me the house. But when I had departed to the Ombite (nome),
26    coming again upon the same house, they dwell (in it). Since therefore also in Mecheir of the present year,
27    when I had come to Diospolis and petitioned about the same matters to Herakleides, the then
28    epistates, and they had been summoned through Artemidoros, the assistant, and had received a copy
29    of the memorandum, they did not appear, avoiding trial, thinking that after a sufficient time I, being worn out, would depart hence as before, I request that you order them to be brought before you
30    and, if I demonstrate these things to be so, to compel them to withdraw from the house; and concerning them
31    having forced their way in and having deposited corpses in my house, to give judgment. And this being done, I shall have obtained justice. Farewell.
32    Deinon, who had joined in the suit for those about Horos, taking up the word, asked Hermias
33    whether he had any proof at hand that it is his ancestral (house). And when he answered that he had
34    none, he pointed out that Hermias had vexed those about Horos to no purpose, for the sake of
35    extorting from them and casting them into damages; for the house, he said, had formerly been bought by the
36    parents of those accused together with Horos, from the true owners, according to Egyptian
37    contracts, of which, when they had also been produced: one, made in the 28th year, in Pachon, under Philometor,
38    according to which the father of Psenchonsis and Chonophres, one of the accused,
39    Teephibis, had bought from Helekis and Lobais and Tbaiais and Senerieus and Herieus [and Senosorphibis and Sisois also called Herieus],
40    being seven (persons), 7½ building-cubits from the southern part of the vacant plots belonging
41    to them, of ten cubits; and another, according to which the father of the accused
42    Nechouthes and Hasos and another Nechouthes and their sister Nechouthis,
43    Hasos, had likewise bought from the same persons another 2½ cubits in the same year and month;
44    and another, made in the 35th year, in Mesore, under the same king, according to which likewise had bought
45    the father of the others, Panas and Patous and Pathemis and Harpchemis and Senamounis,
46    Pechytes, from Ammonios and Zbendetis, the share falling to them of the same house,
47    a fourth part, 3⅓ cubits, of which they had also paid the taxes for the purchase-tax on the sale.
48    He added further that one of them, Horos, had bought from Teephibis and Saos and
49    Pechytes, from each of them a seventh part of the house, and that from the times of the deed of sale
50    the parents of the accused, together with Horos, had had ownership of the house
51    as long as they survived; and after their death Horos had further bought
52    also from one of the accused, Nechouthes the younger, another part of the house; and likewise
53    also Thamounis and Mentemes and Chonophres had bought certain parts from the others,
54    and that he and those accused with him have had ownership up to now indisputably,
55    no one at all having laid claim to it (for) 35 years. And having produced also ordinances
56    and a part of the (ordinance) issued in the 26th year concerning the benevolent (privileges), concerning those
57    who have held possession, he said that, even if someone should suppose that they produce no contract, it suffices from the ordinances themselves that the possession
58    accrues to them, especially indeed when they also produce the titles and have held possession (for) many
59    years; while the opponent stands unproven, and moreover has already advanced
60    in years, and neither in former times against their parents nor indeed
61    against themselves has any suit been set up up to the present, and (he) being in no way able
62    to show at all that any of his own parents, or even himself, had dwelt in
63    Diospolis, nor that the house is his ancestral (property), but has confessedly brought the charge for slander and extortion. Such then being the matters
64    concerning them, and Hermias attaching himself to no proof, but on empty assertion
65    alleging it to be ancestral, to Hermias we said not to lay claim to
66    the house, but that those about Horos should keep possession of it as before.

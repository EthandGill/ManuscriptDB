[TRANSLATION]
1     To my lord husband Tiro, Taesis, very many greetings.
2     Before all things I pray to the lord God that I may receive the letter from me (finding) you in health and good cheer. The sweetest son Dekenber greets you and longs for you very much. Since you wrote to me that Pamis
3     is coming to you and is bringing you goods — behold, he has not yet come, so that
4     I too might be free of care, but I am anxious because he has not come.
5     Nor has Sabinianus come to bring me the things you have
6     in Oxyrhynchus. If you find someone going off to
7     Oxyrhynchus, write to him or to the steward (hospes) and to your mother, so that
8     they may quickly send them to me, while there is still water in the
9     canal. For if these two months pass,
10    they will not find water in the canal, and they will no longer
11    be able to come down here. Behold, I wrote to Sabinianus and to the steward, both concerning what I received from Diogenes — eight (sword-)blades and fifty-five myriads — and I received from Pabion forty-five jars and one couch-cover and two solidi, and I received from Silas a bed and two
12    white tunics and a leopard-marked (garment) and five jars of preserved (olives) and a Eulalian quadruple (cloak?).
13    And your brother sent me of radish-oil
14    fifty sextarii by Pamis. These only I received
15    of all (the things). Since I wrote to you that I received twenty-
16    five myriads for the garment (vestis), I did not receive them
17    nor the cloaks. Plores has the receipts for all (of them). Since he told me that "I am giving them to you on this account on that day," for this reason I wrote to you. On the same
18    day I received only the linen tunic. And
19    concerning the mule I cannot send money
20    because of the need, and he himself takes the provision-allowance (annona) for me, and
21    if the goods come from the boat, he himself brings them.
22    And see that you do not neglect, my lord, the things I wrote to you: six
23    minas of purple, and a hanging lamp and a lampstand
24    and a fine hand-basin and two pounds of good incense
25    and two ladles, one small and one large. And
26    behold, I am weaving your cloak. And a keramion of honey send us, and make haste to come quickly to
27    us. And if you cannot come to us quickly, then write to us when you are coming, so that we too may be of good cheer.
28    Argyris and Eiubinos and Triskentia greet you.
29    Your steward (hospes) Alexander greets you, with his wife
30    and children; and I, Alexander, have grown weary writing the letters for you. And the letter was written on Mecheir
31    new-moon (day). And know that Mucianus took the mule and four myriads.

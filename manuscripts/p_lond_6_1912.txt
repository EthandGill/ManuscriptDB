[TRANSLATION]
1     Lucius Aemilius Rectus declares:
2     Since at the reading of the most sacred
3     and most beneficial letter to the city
4     the whole city was not able to be present
5     because of its great number,
6     I have judged it necessary to publish
7     the letter, so that each man individually,
8     reading it, may both marvel at the majesty
9     of our god Caesar
10    and have gratitude for his goodwill
11    toward the city. Year 2 of Tiberius Claudius
12    Caesar Augustus Germanicus Imperator,
13    the 14th of the month New Augustus.
14    Tiberius Claudius Caesar Augustus Germanicus Imperator, Pontifex
15    Maximus, holding the tribunician power, consul designate, to the city
16    of the Alexandrians, greeting. Tiberius Claudius Barbillus, Apollonis son of Artemidoros,
17    Chairemon son of Leonidas, Marcus Julius Asklepiades, Gaius Julius Dionysios,
18    Tiberius Claudius Phanias, Pasion son of Potamon, Dionysios son of Sabbion,
19    Tiberius Claudius Apollonis son of Ariston, Gaius Julius Apollonios, Hermaiskos
20    son of Apollonios, your envoys, having delivered to me the decree, recounted much concerning
21    the city, drawing my attention thereby to the goodwill toward you
22    which, you may be sure, has long been stored up with me;
23    for by nature you are reverent toward the Augusti, as
24    has become known to me from many things, and especially zealous toward my
25    house, both showing and receiving zeal — of which, that I may mention the last and pass over the rest, the greatest witness is my brother
26    Germanicus Caesar, who addressed you in more heartfelt words.
27    Therefore I gladly accepted the honours given me by you,
28    although I am not readily disposed toward such things. And first I permit you
29    to keep my birthday as an Augustan (festival) in the manner you yourselves have proposed; and the erections of the statues
30    of myself and my family in their various places I grant; for I see
31    [that] everywhere you have been zealous to set up memorials of your reverence toward my house.
32    Of the two golden statues,
33    the one made (in the name) of Claudian Peace Augusta, just as he proposed
34    and earnestly entreated, my most honoured Barbillus, although I refused
35    because it seemed too burdensome, shall be set up at Rome,
36    and the other, in the manner you think fit, shall be carried in procession on the name-days
37    among you; and let there be carried in the procession with it also a chair
38    fitted out with whatever ornament you wish. It would perhaps be foolish, while accepting such great
39    honours, to refuse to establish a Claudian tribe,
40    and to (refuse) that there be groves throughout the nome of Egypt; therefore these things too
41    I grant you. And if you wish, set up also the equestrian statues of Vitrasius Pollio
42    my procurator. And the erections of the
43    four-horse chariots [which] you wish to consecrate to me [at the en]trances of the country
44    I grant: the one near the place called Taposiris of Libya,
45    the one near Pharos of Alexandria, and the third near Pelousion
46    of Egypt, to be set up; but a high priest of myself and the building of temples
47    I decline, not wishing to be burdensome to the men of my own time,
48    and judging that sacred precincts and such things have been assigned as the exclusive privilege of the gods alone
49    through all time.
50    Concerning the requests which you have been eager to obtain from me, I decide thus: to all who have become ephebes up to
51    my principate I firmly preserve the Alexandrian
52    citizenship, with all the privileges and benefits
53    of the city, except for any who, though born of slaves,
54    contrived to slip in among you to become ephebes; and the other things, no less, I wish to be
55    firm, all that was granted to you both by the prefects before me
56    and by the kings and by the governors, as also the god Augustus confirmed.
57    The neokoroi (temple-wardens) of the temple in Alexandria which is of the god
58    Augustus I wish to be chosen by lot, just as those at Canopus
59    of the same god Augustus are chosen by lot. And concerning the civic offices being three-yearly, you seem to me to have decided very well,
60    for the magistrates, through fear of giving an accounting for what they
61    ruled badly, will conduct themselves more moderately toward you during their time
62    in office. Concerning the council (boule), what was your custom
63    in the time of the ancient kings I cannot say, but that in the time of
64    the Augusti before me you did not have one you well know. Since this is a new
65    matter, now first being instituted, and it is unclear whether it will be of benefit to the city and to my affairs, I have written to Aemilius Rectus
66    to investigate and to make clear to me whether it is right to establish the institution,
67    and the manner, if indeed it should be gathered, in which this shall be done.
68    As for the disturbance and strife against the Jews — or rather, if the truth must be
69    told, the war — though many of your envoys contended eagerly in mutual opposition, and above all Dionysios son of Theon,
70    as to which (party) were the cause,
71    nevertheless
72    I did not wish to examine strictly which were responsible, reserving for myself
73    an unrelenting anger against those who begin again;
74    and I declare plainly that, unless you cease this destructive and obstinate anger against one another, I shall be forced
75    to show what a benevolent governor is when turned to righteous anger. Therefore even now I solemnly charge that the Alexandrians, on the one hand,
76    behave gently and humanely toward the Jews who
77    have inhabited the same city for many years,
78    and in no way profane the rites observed by them for the worship
79    of their god, but allow them to follow
80    their customs as they did in the time of the god Augustus, which I too,
81    having heard both sides, have confirmed; and the Jews, on the other hand,
82    I expressly order not to busy themselves about more than they
83    formerly had, nor, as though dwelling in two cities, to send out two embassies in future,
84    which was never done before, nor to force their way into
85    the gymnasiarchic or kosmetic games,
86    while enjoying their own (goods) and reaping in
87    a foreign city the abundance of plentiful blessings,
88    nor to bring in or admit Jews who sail down from Syria or Egypt,
89    from which I shall be compelled to take graver suspicions;
90    otherwise I shall by every
91    means proceed against them as stirring up
92    a kind of general plague of the world. If
93    both parties, desisting from these things, are willing to live together with gentleness
94    and humanity toward one another,
95    I too will take the utmost forethought for the city
96    as one belonging to us from our ancestors of old.
97    To Barbillus my companion I bear witness that he has always taken forethought
98    for you with me, who even now has employed every zeal in the contest on your behalf,
99    and to Tiberius Claudius Archibius my companion.
100   Farewell.

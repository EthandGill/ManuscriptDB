[TRANSLATION]
1     Your ancestral and innate and magnificent blood ever preserves, by edicts, the justice
2     implanted toward all; but most of all it ought, even more,
3     under your so great good-governance, to display to those in need and
4     wishing to obtain consolation for their wrongs, who endure unceasing
5     affliction. Since therefore, master, after the Master God, benefactor, and the …
6     protector of the whole province, since you have been allotted truly blood from the race of Zeus,
7     by the namesake of your ever-remembered and most magnificent parents, we beseech,
8     therefore, supplicating and adjuring by the ever-flowing God your most splendid
9     and most magnificent lordship, to have mercy on those cast from Aphrodite into the public prison.
10    For inasmuch as you are child-loving fathers, you are of all the destitute most compassionate, treasures
11    of the whole province, and nourishers of the poor and of the blind, and tender-hearted
12    givers, that their most wretched children be not constrained by famine. For whenever
13    it befalls the poor man that his hands are tied up, his mouth too is shut off; and for
14    the information of your true teaching, that they are not in the class of tax-payers
15    but are only handicraftsmen, and are not composed of any other (kind of) citizenship — if it should occur to your Height — let each exercise himself in his own occupation, since indeed
16    they are fullers and bronze-smiths and carpenters and barge-makers, and they have no other handiwork from (their) parents
17    and forebears, except the acquired (skill) of such a craft. Once, then, master, the due
18    correction they have undergone, many days already having passed in (the place of) labor.
19    May you be entreated by your innate love, after God, to be moved
20    toward them, and to command that these be released, first because of
21    your soul-profit, and because of the great moderation that holds them,
22    and the starvation of their children. It is profitable
23    also, this, for the public account, that each one, having completed the
24    local public services of his craft, when the season calls (him) to the customary farming
25    … needs … for that same farming (needs) the gathering-together of each of them
26    … is … most of all … o
27    … and … from somewhere their seed to be well-tended.
28    Let me not, then, fail of (this) entreaty and request, the safety
29    of the dearest … and aei…
30    p … about anything whatsoever earnest [to God], a daring deed
31    most daringly I dared to write, not … nor nei…
32    but supplicating, and (giving) occasions to you as wise (to be) wiser
33    to be, master benefactor, of consular (rank), protector

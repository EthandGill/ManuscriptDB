1     Copy [from the official record]s.
2     Year 4 of [Imperator] Caesar Domitian
3     Augustus [Germanicus, M]echeir 14.
4     I[si]dor[os also called Phibion a]gainst Achilleus … …
5     … … …
6     [a] case … to receive (back) …
7     … …
8     Septimius Vegetus … (against) Phibion.
9     Having presented(?) … admitting that he owes you
10    on account of which … at once
11    to be flogged … Phibion: I grant the matter
12    to him, so that I may [be] free of care.
13    Septimius Vegetus: … do you bring a charge against him?
14    Kephalon, advocate: Needing your benefaction, he appeals to you first of all and most necessarily, asking pardon,
15    since he erred concerning the petition; for he ought to have given you a report, as you also wished. We ask therefore that he not be flogged. The matter [now]
16    is this. The father of the opposing party borrowed [from]
17    our father one hundred artabas of wheat. The right is hereditary. And I think that one must not be heir only of the documents, but also repay the debts owed by those whose estate is inherited.
18    [Ar]istonikos, advocate: Let him read out how [these things] are owed.
19    When [Ke]phalon had read out a directive of Archios [also called Poly]deukes in the 11th year of the deified Claudius —
20    Aristonikos: this money-lender(?) …
21    here are summoned those who [have lent] for many years already; the man must be set in the theatres …; but our (client) is a respectable person.
22    … … of the …
23    … when … document …
24    … written … …
25    … …
26    Phibion: I do not know. Septimius Vegetus: what you yourself do not know, neither do we … .
27    Kephalon: the directive belongs to this man's father;
28    but the slave, being his steward, subscribed that
29    he would measure out (the grain), but he has measured out nothing at all from (the time of) the directive. Septimius Vegetus: first
30    it is asked whether the documents belong to this man's father; second, why up to today have you not demanded (it)? for
31    it is possible also that … wrote (it) and measured (it) out.
32    Aristonikos: you ask these things rightly, but I tell you the
33    general (rule): governors fixed a five-year (period)
34    concerning long-standing (debts); but others (fixed) ten years, not where
35    assizes and governors having come (took place). Ask
36    him: how many years did his father survive (after this)?
37    Phibion: this man renounced the inheritance of his father,
38    and I (renounced) that of my own father, since they were sitologoi and were being dunned for the account of Caesar.
39    Aristonikos: if then it was also owed to the account of Caesar,
40    why did he not demand it then? Septimius Vegetus:
41    when a famine occurred, hungering, you did not demand the wheat,
42    if it was owed to you? Phibion: he begged me to take four
43    minas. Aristonikos: if it succeed for this man, ten thousand will bring forward documents of this man's father;
44    for he left him in orphanhood.
45    Septimius Vegetus to Phibion: you indeed deserved to be flogged, having on your own (authority) detained a
46    respectable man and a woman; but I grant you to the crowds and shall be more benevolent toward you. After forty years you bring a directive; the half of
47    the time I grant you; after twenty years you shall come back to me. And he ordered the document to be crossed out (cancelled).

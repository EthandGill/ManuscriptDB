[TRANSLATION]
1   Claudius Terentianus to Claudius Tiberianus, his lord [and] father, ve[ry many]
2   greetings.
3   Before all things I pray for your health and good fortune, which is my wish,
4   and I myself am well, making your obeisance every day
5   before the lord Sarapis and the gods who share his temple. I want you to know, father,
6   that I have received from Achillas a little basket. Likewise another little basket
7   was given to me by the soldier, in which I found two large loaves and date-
8   palm dates(?), and from father Julius a little basket and my baskets and
9   a knife-sheath. He sent me word about a wife, taking my opinion (as though)
10   he were buying (her) for me. Long ago, two years back, I would have had (one) in my house,
11   but I did not permit myself, nor do I permit (myself), to take
12   anyone apart from you; and conversely, you have not heard from me about this
13   matter. If there is anywhere a woman whom, if it seem good to me, I may bring (home), one able
14   to be more well-disposed to you on my behalf and to care for you more than I — it is settled
15   that I be grateful to you rather than that you blame me. For this reason, until
16   today no woman has entered my house, except the one whom you may ap-
17   prove. But you yourself know again that, having sorted out your pending affairs, I have …
18   [...] you remain, for your lifetime, immovable; (then) of my own
19   [wi]fe I take leave. But if not, whomever you approve, that one I too wi[sh].
20   By night I wrote you this letter, having found an opportunity,
21   though not being able to send (it) to you. You will do well to write
22   back to me about your welfare and the rep[ly] to (this) letter.
23   Greet all those from the Caesareum by name.
24   Greet Didymos the lawyer with all his household. And you will tell Longinus
25   that I hope to go up again. One thing I thank the gods for, that
26   I gave you more than the eight drachmas; if not, it would have been the same.
27   And if you go up to the Arsinoite (nome), go to Koursilla and re-
28   ceive the five logs of wood, and you will bring them
29   when you come; and if you can, bring us other things for the
30   winter. Epitynchanon greets you.
31   Greet all those who love [us] by name.
32   Deliver to Claudius Tiberianus [...]
33   from Claudius Terentianus, (his) son, soldier of the legion.

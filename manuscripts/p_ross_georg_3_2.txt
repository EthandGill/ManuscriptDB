[TRANSLATION]
1     Serenus to Antonia his mother, best wishes.
2     Having heard, lady, of the death of …tros, I was distressed. But it is human;
3     for indeed we too are pursuing this (fate). And we consoled Markos also greatly, grieving both because of that man's death and
4     because of your grieving. But, the gods willing, since this one survives for you,
5     you have nothing bad. You will therefore do well, mother, taking
6     our letter and immediately coming in to us, knowing that my brother Markos is greatly preoccupied with the sick and with the surgery. You know that it is not
7     easy to leave behind not a few patients and the workshop,
8     lest some grumbling arise against us, and that in such
9     a governorship. Certainly Markos has informed you by letter
10    of my being busy; for I am in a civic liturgy and
11    on this account I am occupied; since it was not possible for me, having sat only a few days, to send him off to you. Considering the concern of Harpokras
12    for both of us and aiming at what is good,
13    we are intent on one thing: that, coming in with your daughter,
14    you may pass the rest of your life here with your son; for indeed
15    the daughter also will be with you, in grief no small consolation,
16    and you have a woman revered, longed-for by many because of the …
17    memory, but much more because of the honor of Markos which he has here,
18    knowing that through the leading men of the village and the well-disposed here — rather, those concerned for Markos — we have been mediated for since his former journey abroad, and
19    through friends and through the ancestral gods and through letters, that we might not be torn apart from one another. Therefore then,
20    mother, as a sensible woman, taking our letter
21    from Harpokras, immediately having set your own affairs in order,
22    take thought for (joining) us; for you can, your own property
23    partly putting in lease, partly in safekeeping, thus hasten to us.
24    Be of good cheer, lady.

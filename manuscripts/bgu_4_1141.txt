[TRANSLATION]
1   To Erosis(?), very many gree(tings). I received from Ph[i]lox[enos] [yo]ur letter,
2   only it (and)
3   … we have been at odds since … . Wherefore also [with]
4   much [j]oy I detained them […] … and these (men),
5   and to do the necessary things …
6   is treated humanely. You sent, then, both Ph[ilóxe]nos and Hilaros, to find out
7   as to what I wrote you, whether it is so or not. You seem to have lost your wits, since you compel me, fool that I am, to write to you —
8   (but) I will no longer write you anything, [so that] you may understand that in my first
9   letter there is no fault; for I am not doing the work of an in-
10   former, nor do I think you hold me in an informer’s place. So I ask
11   you and entreat you and adjure you by the Fortune of Caesar — and [thu]s may I
12   see you free — that you laid the letter aside in anger. Ask those
13   you have sent, item by item, and I have given true proofs.
14   You are absurd, writing that, if Eros satisfies you, I should write you; and by writ-
15   ing to him to insult me, you wrote this to mock me. As for me, I do not
16   think I deserve to be insulted,
17   for I have done you no wrong, nor will it appear to your friends that I am insulted, satisfying
18   you while you
19   satisfy me. For I trust in myself:
20   since I became your friend, I left no room (for blame). One thing you will charge me with,
21   if you both confer honor on me and want me to be a man …
22   and recommended me both to fellow-slaves and to fellow-freedmen — which to me
23   is wealth, in your eyes — yet I insult the now-rich, beside your
24   fellow-slave and fellow-freedman.
25   For I did not become your friend in order to snatch anything away; rather your
26   own soul knows that, just as a slave on the eve of freedom wishes to please, so
27   I too, wishing your friendship blameless, kept myself so.
28   For what an outrage he did me in the garden and in
29   the house of Terentius, with Priamos and Philoxenos and Hilaros present — were it (possible) to write you tears,
30   I would have written you out of my tears, and …(?)
31   our walk(?) from the garden. About these things those you have sent will make clear,
32   if only they are not willing to do a favor to a fellow-slave.
33   And about Xystos you write me that he is in poor health outside; whether the fellow-slave
34   can recommend him, I do not know, for I do not sleep
35   inside to know. On the days when I go up, I find him sitting
36   and stuffing himself with woof-thread(?); and each day I question the door-keeper
37   whether anyone has slept outside, and the man of the house never once (told) me
38   that anyone had dined outside. But learning that Xystos had dined inside
39   in the house with Eros, twice I took him into the house with me and
40   gave him instructions that there be nothing between him and that man — being cautious,
41   because I had learned beforehand about the little rings that Eros made,
42   lest he persuade him to disclose something in the handling. And about
43   the shadow-cloth(?) it became clear to me, when I investigated with Philoxenos and Hilaros, that the pur-
44   ple had been
45   exchanged by Diodoros and not given to you — against which he showed you
46   a sample — because the old man who was hiding the shadow-cloth, when ques-
47   tioned by me, said it had been exchanged … said to him: “Why from the start did you not disclose these things, so that
48   you too might be treated kindly?” He said: “Diodoros had promised me
49   the kindness,” who neither paid me my wages nor the kindness;
50   wherefore necessity compelled me to inform.” So, having put him to the test,
51   I questioned him privately, unknown to Xystos,
52   wishing to learn whether Xystos too
53   was privy to it; the old man said he knew nothing at all about these matters.
54   And I said to him: “You must also give a written bond that Xystos was not privy
55   to these things,” who first … him to give a written bond by the cubit-rule …
56   … Diodoros, because …
57   who urges me to await the …
58   to him Diodoros, because he had not given …
59   to him being … (in office?)
60   I am in health …(?) being present …
61   … and to be of use … It seemed good to me, then …
62   … could … the written [bond] …

[TRANSLATION]
1     To [Aurelius] Ammonius, the most distinguished prefect of Egypt,
2     [from Aureli]us Melas son of Herakleides, from the village of Theadelphia in the Arsinoite nome. The acts of lawlessness and reckless daring committed in these regions, my lord governor, are checked by no one else except by
3     [your evil-hating] manliness. Now, when I had betrothed to my son Zoilos the daughter of my divine (kinswoman)
4     … [ ] whose name is Taeus, from her tender age, for a partnership in marriage, and in the meantime her
5     [mother] … having died, and her husband Sakaon having taken up life with another woman,
6     [Ka]moutis by name, having taken the same child away from the time of her mother's death,
7     [I cared for her] in all that time [just as if] my own daughter; and, that I may not say much about the marriage, having summoned my friends I joined the children together and fulfilled all the customary things. Now Sakaon,
8     [by the persuasion] of his own wife Kamoutis, has willed to throw the marriage into confusion, the
9     … [ ] which had taken place, on the pretext of the wedding-gifts, as though he had not received them; and from this, having seized the girl,
10    [he keeps her with] himself. Since then this my son Zoilos happens to be a grain-collector, the village
11    [being] utterly [deserted], he brings in no small yearly tax to the most sacred treasury, and from this
12    [fearing] lest he take to flight because of the extortion committed against him … the (?) upon these regions, in writing I gave testimony through the documents which I submitted to the
13    [praepositus] of the pagus, [Ca]storion, and moreover I also gave the same testimony publicly, so that through the
14    [logistes'] trustworthy report it might be reported to your not-to-be-despised manliness. And when mediators had come forward for this, [Arseniu]s son of Kastor from the village of Dionysias, and Apollon and Anoup a soldier
15    … [ ]us the princeps of the camps there, on condition that he receive from me talents of silver … [ and] restore the child to the man for the marriage — and, by your (?) …
16    [this I did]: having borrowed from the tribune Taxumas stationed there, I paid back the
17    [silver, in the presence] of the same witnesses, the aforesaid Arsenius having signed for me on the loan
18    in my stead; and when I asked for the child upon a postponement of days, he kept slandering such a thing,
19    [until the] appointed term expired — and I was given to understand that Kamoutis wished to give the same wife of
20    [Zoilos] in a partnership of marriage to her own nephew Sarmates. And since I was cautious, lest from this [an occasion] of assault should arise and one side or the other should take up the trial concerning life, on this account it was necessary for me
21    [concerning this] to refer the matter to your knowledge, asking that you order, through your sacred subscription, in providence, if it seem right to your [greatness], that Sakaon be compelled to restore my son's wife, or else evidently to come
22    [himself into] your undefiled court; and forever I shall give thanks to your fortune. Farewell.
23    [Aurelius Melas] have submitted (this). Aurelius Ho[ri]on wrote on behalf of him, being illiterate.
24    [To our lords Consta]ntine and Licinius, the Augusti, in their second consulship, on the 16th day before the Kalends of September, Mesore 24.
25    [If] the child [is content] with cohabitation with the man, let this very thing be made manifest before the lo[gistes, in accordance] with the laws. Sheet 26 of the roll … .

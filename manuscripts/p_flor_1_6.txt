[TRANSLATION]
1     To Caluentius Adiutor, the most excellent dioiketes,
2     from Didymos son of Maecenas, former kosmetes and former gymnasiarch, councillor of the great ancient and glorious city of Hermou polis. Philammon the strategos of the nome instructed me, by your command, to come down to Alexandria within the thirtieth of Epeiph, on the petition of Petronius Leios, since I wish to prosecute, there being no public
3     prosecutor, nor having (he) secured to the treasury the fine for false accusation; I wished therefore, if it were possible, to set out at once and partake of your jurisdiction, but since the necessity of the public measurings of the wheat detains me in the nome; for some I measure myself, being not a little, and the cultivators owing
4     to me I compel to measure on my behalf; and I remind you, knowing that the most illustrious prefect Subatianus Aquila too, on account of the inevitable nature of the harvest, also sent back those
5     formerly in another (district) to their own nomes for the
6     time of the harvest; and in addition to these things I set forth a greater plea of justice,
7     requesting another deadline for the voyage down; for I was elected inspector together with others of land being leased out by [the most excellent council] throughout the whole nome belonging to the most sacred treasury … we need more time
8     for the completion; whence I request the present (delay) to be granted; and he happens to have accused me of having pledged in writing to come down, when I never at any time
9     [pledged] in writing. Farewell. And for the delivery of the petition
10    I sent Nikodemos, a councillor, a friend. Didymos, former kosmetes and former gymnasiarch, councillor.
11    Year 18, Epeiph 29.
12    The things ordered by the most illustrious prefect it is fitting to accomplish with speed.
13    If then, the prosecutor having petitioned, you being summoned do not obey, the consequences will follow. Deliver.

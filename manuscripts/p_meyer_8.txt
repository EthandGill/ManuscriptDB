[TRANSLATION]
1     To Lucius Trebius Proclus, the most excellent epistrategos,
2     from Philippos and Charition, both children of Aphrodisios, son of Phi[lippos, a katoikos]
3     of the 6,475 Greek men in the Arsinoite nome, and Charition acting with [me as her guardian,]
4     Philippos. The mother of us both, Didymarion, died long ago, from the … [year]
5     of the deified Hadrian, with us, then minors, as her heirs; and according to [the laws we received as our lot]
6     the property and the buildings and four slave bodies and th[eir off]spring and all the deposited goods. But our aforementioned [father Aphrodi]sios, laying claim to everything, later, since he had united with Sara[pias also called A]thenarion, and as … became of everything, unreasonably demanding …
7     none of our own property, [and at the same] time despising the helpless weakness that is ours.
8     From that time, then, until [now], with him laying hold of everything and [claiming it for himself] and collecting the rents of the slaves, he arranged for everything [to be registered]
9     in the name of his wife, although our father had become a grain-receiver and had been reduced to destitution. We ourselves, then, having become destitute, because I, Philippos, was engaged in public services, and out of necessity making our refuge to you the benefactor, [we ask]
10    that our own property be restored to us together with the use of the whole period, and further
11    also the offspring of the slaves, [so that] we may be helped by you. [Farewell.]
12    Philippos and Charition, both children of Aphrodisios, have submitted this. I, Phili[ppos, wrote also]
13    on behalf of my sister, who does not know letters. In the 14th year of Imperator Caesar T[itus Aelius Hadrianus]
14    Antoninus Augustus Pius, Mesore 23.
15    [Year] 14, Mesore 23.
16    After the sowing, present yourself to me.

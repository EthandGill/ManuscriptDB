3     by the greatest decrees. But here I happen, twelve days ago, more or less,
4     to have been prosecuted by the same revered expeller (exactor) of the Antaiopolite (nome), and in no way granted leisure either for the auspicious transport-tax (embole) or for the [other] public (dues) … nor at all having any access

7     also the receipt of the transport-tax of the most pious monk again through our own man
8     sending to the office, so that the expeller has no[…]
9     through us. But neither were they [ev]er troubled on behalf of our village, either he himself or other
10    expellers before him. And since I have heard that some (charge) has been brought against me
11    by Nepos the singularis, on the suggestion, as has been said, of the same revered Aphthonios, I am ready,
12    if … … the same reve[r]ed sing[ulari]s Nepos … [and] should prove anything
13    of the (charges) brought by him to have been done by me, to submit to the things commanded
14    by your authority. For that nothing of this kind I ever either contemplated or carried out,
15    since I gave orders to (other) men, not to say to the head-man of the village, trusting in myself
16    this very thing I set down. And I beseech, if it please your authority,
17    that the customary statement be ordered to be given to me, which all those of our province possess who are
18    private persons, not to say those handling public (affairs); for he himself who brought these (charges)
19    against me, being himself under accusation, brought false (charges) against [m]e, wishing [m]e, by the suggestion
20    of Aphthonios, to be prosecuted toward the cancellation of the auspicious transport-tax (embole) and of the other public (dues),
21    so that I may be able to make the journeys and reach your
22    court, and to demonstrate myself blameless of all the (charges) vainly
23    accused against me by those wishing to slander me and
24    to scatter the public (revenue).

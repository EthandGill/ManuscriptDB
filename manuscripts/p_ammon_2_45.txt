[TRANSLATION]
1     [A long] time ago this [brother] of mine, Harpokration by name, (devoted) to studies (rhetoric),
2     [himself too eager (for) a journey] abroad outside [of Egypt,] happens
3     to have set out. From there, journeying abroad, he left his own slaves
4     in this city, with me, who at that time was staying (here) before and (for) some other time too. For he expected
5     to return here after a short time. But, having been on that journey abroad,
6     and moving each time from country to country, from Greece
7     to Rome and from Rome to Constantinople and from one (place) to another, on account of
8     the occasion of his studies, he extended the journey abroad over a long time.
9     And in the meantime, neither did he [quickly] return, and I, ever expecting
10    his return from the letters which he sent each time making this clear,
11    his slaves, those in this city, living by themselves, free and as if without a patron, seemed to be (so) because of the long-lasting journey abroad of their master. A rumor
12    therefore having been spread abroad a little while ago, that he had died — for a short time ago
13    I had heard these things, for not quickly does the report concerning the death of those abroad reach the hearing of (their) kin —
14    I prepared, upon this report, to sail down here
15    on account of these things, our slaves staying here. But while I was on the journey,
16    someone comes … announcing that someone arriving from outside, Eugenios by name, said that these
17    slaves in Alexandria belonging to my brother Harpokration, [as being now] masterless
18    and as (if) he died with no heirs, by petition had obtained
19    from the divinity of our triumphant lords, and had obtained a rescript
20    of this sort according to the laws: if no one should claim these (slaves) nor anyone appear
21    defending the ownership of these (slaves), [(they were) to be handed over to him.] [Having heard this in the Thebaid,]
22    I hastened all the more to the journey. But before my arrival
23    in Alexandria … having come, and having learned that that Harpokration
24    had as heir a [brother] both of the same father and of the same mother, namely me, Ammon,
25    [Nike, the mother of these persons (slaves), testifying, he straightway] ran up to the Thebaid, [perhaps] wishing [to] inquire (about) this; and he said that he had letters
26    from my lord Sisinnios calling me to the city of the Alexandrians on account of
27    these slaves, (me) who even before this man's arrival had already prepared to
28    sail down here for this very reason. Since therefore these slaves were guarded with
29    the office of the catholicus (financial administration) until my arrival, Eugenios having both arrested (them) and requested that they be under guard, and (since) he no longer has [anything to dispute] toward me,
30    Eugenios (no longer) has (anything), I request, lord, that these (slaves) be handed over to me and be released from
31    this guard, for the laws now call me to the ownership of these (slaves),
32    being a brother of that Harpokration, of the same father and the same mother, and as
33    the things inscribed in the [divine] rescript given to Eugenios contain, (me) keeping the
34    following of the laws.

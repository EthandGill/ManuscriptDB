[TRANSLATION]
1     To Avidius Hel[iod]oros, the most excellent prefect,
2     Ptolemaios, strategos of the Koptite (nome), greeting.
3     Since the imperial affairs, greatest prefect,
4     require exceptional attention and continuous diligence, and (since) for this there have been given not only
5     trustworthy but also most especially disobedient men, I did not cease, lord, exhorting the Romans and Alexandrians who are in the public
6     services of the nome
7     and the veteran-soldiers who oppose the affairs,
8     to obey the orders, and thinking that they would change their minds, I was holding back from declaring (it) to you the lord. But I persisted with them as far as necessary, being pressed by the need to make report.
9     For of the affairs the greatest and most genuine, requiring much constant attention,
10    [are the e]xactions of the sums owed to the imperial
11    account. On account of which I am vigilant, applying myself
12    to the collection both at hand, and, for the audit each month, summoning
13    the collectors, I examine them with respect to the incoming (collector) on behalf of his own collectorship, demanding the account to give full satisfaction … …
14    [I] owe … [fr]om them. … [grant(?)] …
15    [u]rging … …
16    having been entrusted with another public service,
17    which they ought to obey, and to me
18    when I demand they ought to obey, having welcomed (the claim)
19    that they themselves are not under the strategia,
20    nor yet ought to stand on equal footing with the local
21    collectors to make payment, and if
22    he should impose a penalty, the same men, saying
23    that, as far as in them lies, they hinder the collections, and in some places they even dare
24    to resist; and in general, all such as are
25    of this kind in the public services attempt to go as far as effrontery, so that
26    they seem to set before those of the nome an example of
27    disobedience. Since, then,
28    the imperial affairs are thus hindered, I ask, lord — not few
29    persons being endangered by this — if
30    it seem good to you, to decide concerning them and whatever
31    you may approve to establish as the manner of proceeding against them, it is necessary, so that neither
32    the exaction of the public (taxes) be hindered … the imperial affairs
33    … [at the right] time … and … …
34    (so that) those appointed to the public services
35    and not willing to obey, like the other men of affairs, you may be able to present
36    to the most excellent epistrategos, who will compel
37    them to carry out the duties belonging to them.
38    Year 2, Pharmouthi 4.

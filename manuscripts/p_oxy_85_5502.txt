[TRANSLATION]
1     We were sent from the public (treasury) by Isid[o]rus, strategos of the Oxyrhynchite nome,
2     for the joint-purchase of camels for the service of the public spices, so as to Coptos, by lett[er of the] former go[v]ernor Longaeus Rufus, eight and a half talents; and there were writt[en] b[y the] same strategos letters of introduction to his friends in … the opponent Thracidas, to whom also the
3     letter, having delivered (it), [we handed ove]r not only the eight and a half talents
4     but also (my) own mon[ey, in one talent] three thousand drachmas, so that the
5     whole with him was [ten] talan[ts] … [wh]en we had been sent for this
6     because we were experien[ced] … he announced to us from
7     having come from Taposiris … he contrived, wishing to destroy us by … and persuaded us to
8     go to Cyrene … to make the joint-purchase; and we went round m[any pla]ces in the outlying parts, so that
9     we even went to Cyrene. And [when] we saw him, as time went on, neither making the joint-pur[cha]se of the camels nor
10    repaying the money to us but leading us about in much wandering, we brought suit
11    against him before the procurator of our lord there, K…ius Priscus, who, out of his own diligence, hating wickedness at (the way) in which he had treated us with much running-about of the country, exacted, after the five thousand denarii previously repaid by the opponent, another seven thousand denarii, so that there remained with him three thousand denarii toward the completion of the aforesaid ten talents and more, as the memoranda of the most excellent
12    procurator contain, which he promised to (repay?) to us here with him, aποκα(?) …

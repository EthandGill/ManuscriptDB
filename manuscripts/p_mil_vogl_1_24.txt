[TRANSLATION]
1     To Pa[ul]us my lord and brother,
2     ve[ry] many greetings.
3     Before all things I pray that you are strong and prosper, which is for me a matter of prayer, that you
4     be strong as my revered lord and brother. You write to m[e] saying: if you are able,
5     sail up. By your safety, I swear, and
6     that of my little child, and my uprightness, of which
7     I am persuaded that you care no less than I,
8     I should wish to do nothing else but to
9     worship your face at your soil (in your land),
10    but I have not been able, nor am I able; for now
11    again I have been storm-tossed by Apollotas;
12    for whenever they take wing at my expense, while I
13    toil out at sea, and grow rich, then forgetting what sort they were
14    with me, then and now what sort they are, they trample on me the more, just as also the strong hawk
15    then did again together with him. So now
16    therefore Demetrios, having come to me, from his own observation beheld me weeping with very many
17    tears. What then is it? I, being persuaded
18    in my mind, conscious of nothing bad or wicked,
19    think that I suffer nothing; so whatever
20    therefore I observe how my little affairs proceed, I will again make clear to you, lord; for I know that
21    you hold this as on your own behalf. You need not now
22    get to know me anew. So Demetrios just now told me to go out
23    himself, and I blamed him because for so many days he had not been seen by me, and he said he was distracted about silverware with Kertos. I pray
24    that you are strong. Concerning Ptollarion, his mother
25    complains. She does not speak to me, but even flees
26    me if she sees me, but she reviles him to my people. I rebuke Ptollarion; he says to me
27    that he is reviled by her
28    for the most part unjustly and
29    insolently. I said:
30    I am already an old man of … years
31    and she speaks and reviles as though to a baby infant; but if you put up with her,
32    is she not your mother in
33    manner, who is also a grandmother? I have no strength to listen, face to face, while they argue it out,
34    so that I may recognize the one doing wrong. I know indeed that she is very sharp-tempered
35    and faint-hearted,
36    but this one does not bear
37    perhaps her sharpness. I
38    therefore, if I enter into [the]
39    thirty-couch dining-hall, I see him as often
40    as I enter. And
41    Patron, untouched by the evil eye, has been working hard; he turned out exceedingly sharp.
42    Farewell, lord, together with my
43    lady, your wife Politta, and greet her many times. My wife
44    Chairemonis greets you, and Pollitta who is also Sarapias likewise
45    greets you both.
46    Year 2 of Trajan Hadrian
47    Augustus, Choiak 11.
48    To Paulus.

[TRANSLATION]
1     To King Ptolemy and Queen Cleopatra, his sister,
2     Hail, divine Philometores. Thauês and Taous, the twin priestesses, those serving
3     in the great Serapeum near Memphis. And previously indeed to you
4     [when you] visited in Memphis and went up into the temple to sacrifice,
5     we petitioned and submitted a petition claiming that we were not receiving
6     the maintenance allowance owed us to be given from both
7     the Serapeum and the Asclepieum. But until now we have not received [them] in full,
8     driven by necessity and pressed, as if
9     by hunger being undone, again to petition you, and through few [words]
10    [we set forth] the selfishness of those wronging us. For since you are establishing
11    still from former times a maintenance allowance to both the Serapeum
12    and the Asclepieum, and from these and from the previously born
13    twins having received their daily allowances, and to us
14    when you went up at the outset to the temple, indeed at first for a few days
15    they showed [as if] our proper [allowances] would be withheld,
16    but the rest of the time they did not provide [them]. Therefore also to the
17    administrators we sent those who would petition [on our behalf]
18    and to you, according to the visits you made in Memphis, [we] made [the matter] plain
19    concerning these matters. But those in charge of administration in the Serapeum
20    and Asclepieum, being stationed [there], having greatly ventured, and the [things]
21    being allocated to us from you being carried off, and showing no
22    foresight, but us being pressed by necessities
23    and to Achomarrês indeed the overseer of the temple many times we made requests
24    to return to us, and to the son of Psintaeus, the overseer of the
25    sacred [offices], having gone up recently to the temple, we approached, and about each [matter]
26    we communicated [our concerns]. And summoning Achomarrês
27    he ordered [him] to return to us the things owed. But he, most
28    ungrateful of men, to us indeed promised
29    to complete the matter, but [when] the son of Psintaeus from
30    Memphis having departed, no longer
31    did he take any account. Not only this [man],
32    but also others of those from the Serapeum
33    and others of those from the Asclepieum
34    being in charge of administration, from whom it is the custom
35    for us to receive the necessities, are depriving [us], whose both names and the things owed
36    on account of being numerous we did not judge [it fitting] to record. We beseech therefore you one [thing],
37    having one hope, the support from you to come, to send forth our
38    petition to Dionysios of your friends
39    and general, so that he might write to Apollonios
40    the superintendent, having received from us
41    the record of the things owed to us
42    of necessities and which [items] were owed to which [persons] at what times
43    have been owed additionally and by whom [...]
44    to force them to return [things] to us,
45    in order that possessing everything henceforth much more
46    the customary rites to Sarapis and to Isis
47    we may perform in behalf of both you and
48    your children. But may there be for you
49    to rule all whatever land you might choose.
50    Farewell.

[TRANSLATION]
1     It is clear to all that the freedom which from above and from nature has been confirmed
2     to men, neither long time nor error following upon it
3     can diminish. For Jacob, originating from the Antaiopolite nome, and having spent with my father, of holy memory,
4     no little time, rendered him very much service. And Sophia also lived alongside him,
5     that man's most kindly wife, remaining together with her aforenamed husband
6     Jacob in the service of my master of holy memory, my father of illustrious memory. And no document ever
7     did I find from my father, [nor] any contract, nor from anyone else, nor any contract, that they have stood as slaves from any
8     lawful title whatever. From those aforenamed Jacob and Sophia, then, children
9     came to be: Leah and Rachel and Rebecca. From Leah is born a child, Markos; and
10    … Markos and [Sophia, but] Leah came to the end of her life, as the divine will willed, together with her son Markos. And Sophia,
11    the survivor, daughter of this departed Leah, bears John
12    and Isaac and Jacob and, from a certain freeman … others, dragging her together with the same children
13    into slavery, shall see, when they come before the most fearful tribunal of the Mightier One. And these things concerning this.
14    [Since] then of those aforementioned Jacob and Sophia, as above
15    has been shown, were born Rachel and Rebecca and Leah, and Rachel chose the solitary life,
16    but Leah bears Sophia the now surviving, and Rebecca bears Eulogia and Martha. But Eulogia chose the ascetic
17    [life]; and there remains Martha, the sister of Eulogia, daughter of Rebecca of blessed memory, who chose the solitary life, she too,
18    living in my house. And so that not in some time
19    by someone of those who, as is likely, succeed to my rights, should grow old my most learned
20    heir — but that the divine should protect for the longest time my most learned son and heir Victor, enjoying his own property — so that then
21    Martha be not harassed as though she were a slave, for this reason I have come to this present
22    written acknowledgment, through which I both intend her, and accurately know, not to be
23    at all a slave, nor to have been born of a slave-woman, in the manner I have set forth above;
24    and if, as is likely, someone of those after these things should [attempt to trouble her, from this]
25    [written acknowledgment, to which I have prepared also to consent the most learned, with God's help,]
26    [my heir, through which I urge her from now already to transact the affairs of the free]
27    and the things befitting the free. For I have heard her at the present time
28    speaking and being questioned about a certain matter belonging to her, as though I were [not]
29    free. And for this reason, fearing the judgment of God and considering the [loving-kindness]
30    [of] the Savior, I groaned, and to [the] present … [I] came.

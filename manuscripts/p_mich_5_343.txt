[TRANSLATION]
1     [Chrates son of Eutychos and Eutycho]s son of Chrates, the two of them Persians of the epigone, and the wife of Eutychos and mother of me, Chrates, Theokla daughter of Kronion, Persian woman, with her guardian Eutychos himself, the three of us, mutual sureties [for] payment, agree
2     [that we have received from Horion ] … at once as a dowry, on behalf of his own sister Kroniaina, of coined silver of stamped money two thousand one hundred sixty drachmas, and as parapherna, without valuation: a gold chain
3     … of four quarters, both of the gold of Alexandrian and of local assayed gold; a gold earring of four quarters, and a gold crescent of three quarters, and six … and a pair of silver bracelets of the weight of twenty drachmas of unstamped (silver), and a silver armlet of the weight of eight drachmas of unstamped (silver), and a silver spoon of the weight of four drachmas, and bronze vessels … and a soap-box and a folding mirror and six little water-jars, all of bronze, and women's vessels of tin of the weight of twelve minas, and a cypress-wood chest
4     … [and three] women's robes, one white, the second ivy-colored, and the third scarlet, and ten cloaks mixed in their colors; and as an additional gift, without valuation, from the slave bodies belonging to him, a house-born slave girl named Ammounous, born from Thaubas the slave-woman, aged five years, whom Horion shall deliver healthy and unharmed in the month Kaisarios of the [fifteenth year of Tiberius Cl]audius Caesar Augustus Germanicus Imperator. And he shall treat Kroniaina as a wedded wife, and if a separation occurs we shall return the dowry and the parapherna. And the
5     [two, both Eutychos and Theokla,] consent that I, Chrates, may set apart for myself, after the death of Eutychos himself, (a share) from the property belonging to Eutychos.

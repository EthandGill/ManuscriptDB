1     … …tos and that he
2     has (claims) about certain matters against her, Lupus said: concerning
3     … the strategos
4     of the city I give you as judge.
5     Year 20 of the deified Trajan, Tybi the tenth.
6     When Lucia Macrina, through Phaneios the advocate, stated
7     that she demanded back a deposit from the property
8     of Antonius Germanus, a deceased soldier, Lupus said: we understand that the deposits are dowries. On such grounds
9     I do not grant a judge. For it is not permitted for a soldier
10    to marry. But if you demand back a dowry, by granting a judge
11    I shall seem to be persuaded that the marriage is lawful.
12    From a remission of Mamertinus:
13    Year 18 of Hadrian the lord, Phamenoth 1, in
14    Koptos, Chthinbois against Casius Gemellus,
15    a cavalryman of the Boukontioi, in the presence of her father
16    Orestoouphis. When Apolinarios the advocate stated that, of (sums) owed by Gemellus to Tinbois,
17    seven hundred drachmas according to a bank-deed, he should pursue this in accordance with the deed,
18    and Alexandros and Herakleides, the advocates, answered that Chthinbois, as having been married to him,
19    wished to demand back from him the dowry which she had given him, and could neither receive (it)
20    since it is forbidden for soldiers [to take]
21    wives, and that her father, having [acted as guardian] for him, when accounts were settled [with] him,
22    had advanced to him documents… that he had kept in this account… concerning
23    the dowry, having been entrusted… from the
24    mine a dwelling… of him, and very many other things, and concerning the embezzlement, that the accused (woman)… had received the
25    arrangement of the account; (and) that he therefore asked that what she had embezzled
26    she be compelled to repay in turn,
27    when the advocate (for her)… having brought forward… that she had embezzled nothing
28    of Gemellus's, but had lent to him
29    according to two bank-deeds the seven hundred drachmas, by the first 260 and by the
30    second 440, and was demanding these back not as (from) a husband but as
31    from a debtor; the bank-deeds having been read, [the priest] and
32    archidikastes (said): to soldiers it is [forbidden] to take wives …
33    but them not even one …
34    the second [bank-]deed not containing (anything) to be referred to the account of a dowry, the 440, …
35    to repay these to her; … but according to the second bank-deed … the 260 cannot be retained, since these have the rights of a dowry. When Gemellus asked
36    that there be reserved for him an account of the embezzlement against
37    Chthinbois …, Ulpius Asklepiades, former prefect of the second cohort
38    of the Hispani, the priest and archidikastes (said): if
39    you have any clear proofs,
40    if you bring them forward, I will hear you.
41    Year 18 of Trajan, Phaophi the twenty-seventh. When Longinus
42    …us stated that, being himself a Roman, he had served [in] the first cohort of the Thebans under Severus, and had cohabited during his service
43    with a Roman woman, by whom he had begotten
44    Longinus Apolinarios and Longinius Pomponius, whom he asks to be adjudged (legitimate),
45    Lupus, having spoken with the legal experts, said:
46    [since] the children have been registered as born [from] a Roman woman,
47    you both wish to leave them legitimate by adjudication, but
48    I cannot make their father lawful (i.e. their lawful father).
49    Year 18 of Trajan, Pauni 10.
50    When Chrotis, through Philoxenos the advocate, stated
51    that, being herself a townswoman, she had united with Isidoros a townsman, and after these things, that one having served as a soldier
52    in a cohort, she had by him a son Theodoros, concerning whom she petitions, asking whether, if it was neglected, a birth-certificate of him should be filed, and that he is a son
53    of that man (is clear) from a will which he wrote — that it is plain, for he left him heir of his own (property); a will of Iulius
54    Martialis, a soldier of the first cohort of the Thebans, having been read, Lupus, having spoken with the
55    friends (assessors), said: Martialis
56    while serving could not have a lawful son,
57    but he wrote him as heir lawfully.
58    Year 5 of Antoninus the lord, the 3rd intercalary day.
59    When Octavius Valens and Casia Secunda came forward, having been deferred from the day before,
60    Eudaimon, having taken counsel with those present,
61    said: indeed yesterday, immediately after the minutes of the most excellent Heliodoros had been read, and the cause for which it had been deferred had become plain,
62    (I had) to see concerning a matter of forbidden (things),
63    the mother of this child having petitioned,
64    and today, having examined the matters pertaining to this, I confirm what yesterday I had supposed. Of one going out (on service) whether in a unit (taxis) or in a cohort or in a cavalry squadron (ile), the one begotten cannot be a lawful son. And not being
65    a lawful son, while the father is an Alexandrian, he cannot be an Alexandrian. This child was begotten to Valens while serving in a cohort; he is alien
66    to him; he cannot be admitted into the citizenship of the Alexandrians. And he added: yesterday you said
67    that you had other children; of what age are they, when
68    were they born? Octavius Valens answered:
69    one now, and one is older. Eudaimon
70    said: the older — where was he born to you while you were serving (as a soldier)?
71    Valens answered: in a cohort, and this younger one (likewise). Eudaimon said: know
72    that those too are of the same status as this one.
73    Some things are inviolable. Valens said: just now, if it happen that I go abroad, you yourself will subscribe for me
74    through an agent that I receive my rights. What wrong have
75    the children done? Eudaimon said: I have acted foolishly
76    in saying through many (words) what I could … to accomplish in the briefest. Since then you attempt the impossible,
77    neither this one nor your other sons are citizens
78    of the Alexandrians.

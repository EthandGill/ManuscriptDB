[TRANSLATION]
1     Dionys[ios to Ptolem]y: greetings and
2     be well. Such freedom of myself
3     I have displayed to all human beings,
4     most especially to you and your brother,
5     through both Sarapis and your
6     freedom, and I have tried [it],
7     from when you were established with me, in everything
8     useful to you, offering myself.
9     When your brother met with me
10    on the seventeenth of Mechir
11    and asked me, so that, if he bring [it],
12    [a] third[-portion], let those
13    from me the scribes receive for him all the
14    accounts, I said to him: do not
15    ask me, but [rather] your brother
16    seeming to be in the court
17    to come; to monographs nothing give to write
18    nor to spend bronze, and I dismissed [him],
19    saying to him: come earlier, so that
20    having taken from me in Memphis sesame
21    a quarter, grind for me in Memphis [a] mill-product
22    on account of wishing to give [it]
23    to carry into the city. And that one appears
24    that day having been occupied [or] was ashamed
25    to mix with me. If then this is
26    the reason and through this no longer he comes to me,
27    ashamed, calling him, send
28    to me, for [there] is shame [for me].
29    For I myself by the gods
30    am anxious, lest [perhaps the] boy
31    is ill, and I do not have leisure to go up to you.
32    But if for some other reason he does not appear to me, know
33    clearly, that if I go up and I myself come before
34    you, I shall not enter, but rather
35    to the inn of Protagoras I shall put up. Be well,
36    [… ]
37    [Farewell.] …
38    To Ptolemy.

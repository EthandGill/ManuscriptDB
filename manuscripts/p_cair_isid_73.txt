[TRANSLATION]
1     To J[ulius] Julianus, the most eminent praefect of E[gyp]t,
2     fr[om the Aurelii Is]idoros son of Ptolemaios, tesserarius, and Palem[on] son of Tiberinos, quadrarius, both from the village of Karanis of the Arsinoite nome. … air …
3     W[e the] poorer country-folk suffer terrible things both at the hands of the praepositus of the pagus, Theodoros, and of the village-headmen (komarchs). They afflict us, which
4     shows the disposition of these utterly wicked men: these, lording it over the (region), and (whereas) they ought to have feared the magnitude of you[r authority], having made a conspiracy against us, have also made exactions as they please — very many unlawful assessments in the village, the village-headmen together [with the prae]positus, more than three hundred talents, [and] these we know not to what account they have gone. We wish therefore that [you] know (it), [and] through your hatred-of-evil [to] proceed to the punishment of these (men). But indeed, also from the price of hides and the price of a camel, six talents of silver, and from the price of a horse,
5     fif[ty] talents, so that of these too there comes to the same amount fifty-six talents of sil[ver], and from surplus, exactions of ten artabas, and these into their
6     own (pockets) [they carried] off. And the aforesaid praeposi[tus] Theodoros, prevailing, (took) into his own house two female beasts of burden, bough[t]
7     by the village for forty [tale]nts of silver, [and] he keeps them with himself to this day; and of the [same female] beasts, two, one
8     he has sold to us for twenty-seven talents of silver fo[r] business purposes, reckoning our village to be no[thing; but] also, having shaken down the village, thirty wh[i]te sheep and forty-seven talents of silver, and indeed also our donkey-be[as]ts of the village as corvée-(animals),
9     without higher orders, for the transport of his pleasure-boat (phaselos) to his house, and allowing us no peace, we make our appeal [to you], who have happily arrived in our homeland, for the punishment of the things dared. Since therefore it is not right that disturbances be carried out b[y]
10    the praepositus Theodoros, but also that unreasonable assessments not be made by the village-headmen together with the same praepositus, on this account, not bearing
11    these things, we submit to your virtue these very (documents) in writing, recording (them), begging and entreating that you order, through your sacred subscription,
12    your magnitude to proceed against both the praepositus and the village-headmen concerning what they have dared against us. Farewell.
13    We, Aur[e]lii Isidoros and Palemon, have submitted (this). I, Aurelius Heron son of Melas, from the quarter of Phremei, wrote on their behalf as they are illiterate.

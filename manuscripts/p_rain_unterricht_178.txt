1     Let there be a lune, of which the outer perimeter is
2     [10] schoinia, and the inner perimeter 12 schoinia; the base 2 schoinia. How many arouras is it? Thus you must do it. Add together the
3     two perimeters, it makes 22. Of these take the half,
4     it makes [11]. These schoinia multiply by the two of the base,
5     it makes [22]. So many arouras is the lune,
6     as is set out below.
7     schoinia 12
8     schoinia 2; schoinia 10; schoinia 2
9     the result: 32 arouras (sic)
10    Let there be a circle, of which the perimeter is [30] schoinia. How many arouras is it? Thus you must do it. Multiply the 30 schoinia by themselves, it makes 900.
11    Of these take the twelfth, it makes [75]. So many
12    arouras is the circle, as is set out below.
13    perimeter 30 schoinia
14    the result: 71 arouras (sic)

16    is it? Thus you must do it. Multiply the 10 by themselves,
17    it makes 100. Of these take the [three-quarters], it makes 75. So many arouras
18    is the circle, as is set out below.
19    schoinia 10
20    the result: 75 arouras

[TRANSLATION]
1     Hierokles to Zenon [greetings. If you] are well, it would be good; and we are well. You [wrote]
2     to me about Pyrrhus, whether if we know precisely, to anoint him, but if not, lest wasteful [expense] befall and [be drawn] from the letters. Abo[ut then the-]
3     to know, the gods would most know, but to Ptolemy it appears, as much as [is according to] human [nature],
4     that of those now being anointed, who have obtained much time, much [better...]
5     and exceedingly in little time will far exceed them; and [he] proceeds to [these]
6     and to the remaining studies; and with the gods, to speak, I hope you will be crowned. Send [back]
7     to him a strigilarium as quickly as possible, and especially let the skin be goat[skin], but if not, [calf-]
8     fine, and a tunic and a cloak and the bedding and a spread and [pillows]
9     and the honey. You wrote to me wondering if I do not hold that with all these things an end [follows].
10    I know, but you are able managing so that it may be sent as safely as possible.
11    Hierokles about Pyrrhus.
12    Year 29, Xandikos 3,
13    in Memphis.
14    To Z[e]non.

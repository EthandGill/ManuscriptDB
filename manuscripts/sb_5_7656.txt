[TRANSLATION]
1     And without letters I know that your virtue is from itself and does not need to be reminded concerning matters regarding me, but [of the]
2     lord my brother Neilos, encountering him there, I considered it necessary first to pay the owed greeting, then also to request that your courage deign to
3     arrange my affairs and those belonging to me and not allow anyone to suffer any one of [them]
4     to be mistreated, even though I am absent, but nevertheless I am confident that my affairs are being arranged more through [your]
5     greatness of mind; trusting therefore I have written; but the skins of one whole-hide
6     deign to provide to the house, I mean the fourteen; for I
7     [am trying] to catch the Mareotite and to press upon the soldier-farmers laboring there.
8     Let your virtue know that I was demanded for the six golden pieces, which I pledged
9     to the lord Christ on behalf of your courage; for [he] did not allow me to go [to]
10    Memphis, unless he made me pledge them firmly to Anubian [the]
11    helper of the numerarii and in order that your virtue might know [this] I hastened to write
12    and second this: I sent the boy to bring my garments; if then there is any need,
13    deign your [virtue] to make it known to me through him; for I am hastening to reach Al[exand]r[ia].

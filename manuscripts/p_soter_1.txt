[TRANSLATION]
1     In the first year of the Emperor Titus Flavius
2     Vespasianus Caesar, in the month Kaisareios, on the twelfth, in Theadelphia of the Themistes division of the
3     Arsinoite nome. Aphrodisios son of Apollonios, about sixty years old, with a scar on his right forearm, leased to Soterichos son of Lykos, a Persian of the epigone, about twenty-
4     four years old, with a scar under his chin, the vineyard belonging to him
5     near Theadelphia, trained on trees,
6     of half an aroura or however much it may be. This lease
7     is for three years from the coming second year
8     of the Emperor Titus Flavius Vespasianus
9     Caesar, on the terms that the lessee (shall receive) a third part
10    of the fruits and produce yielded from the vineyard each year, free of public dues and of every kind of impost, there being subtracted
11    in common all the wine-making expense and
12    the wine that will be needed at the wine-press. Let it not then be permitted
13    to the lessee within the term to abandon
14    the lease; but also let him dig the
15    vineyard each year with two diggings, one in winter,
16    a clean four-palm digging, and the other in summer,
17    a single-stroke one; and let him make the pruning of the vine
18    moderate and proper, not cutting long. And in regard to the
19    yearly carrying of earth and manure and sand,
20    Aphrodisios shall furnish the asses, and the lessee
21    shall dig over and scatter (it); and let him work the reeds each year …
22    … [afresh] five-post trellises …
23    … and of the rest the propping, Aphrodisios furnishing reed and ropes; and let the same lessee carry out the yearly drawing of water
24    from the first of Pharmouthi until the coming (season)
25    of irrigation from the foot, one (day) in every two days,
26    and let him bear the expense, providing the embankment-maker; all the fruit-trees in the vineyard
27    being the lessee's, from which let him give each year … and after
28    the term let the lessee hand over the vineyard clean [of rushes]

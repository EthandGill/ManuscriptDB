[TRANSLATION]
1     To Flavius Triadios Marianos Michaelios
2     a plea and supplication from me, Apollos the wretched, from Poucheos
3     of the village of the Antaiopolite nome.
4     A very great benefaction is laid before all who are wronged, namely the redress of your good fame. I therefore,
5     knowing this very thing precisely, approach your glorious and inviolate footsteps,
6     wishing to obtain justice, having fallen into unspeakable losses, with no remedy at hand,
7     at the hands of the most venerable lady Theophile, the wife of the one of illustrious memory, Phoibammon
8     son of Dioskoros son of Ioulios. For my affair stands thus. I inform your most glorious benevolence,
9     which God and the most divine masters of the scepters preferred, appointing it to rule this wretched
10    land of the Thebans, [and] to check the bitter wrongs of those dwelling in it, knowing well its hatred of evil
11    in all things and its more-just conduct in many respects: I happened to be the lessee of the aforesaid (land) of the aforementioned
12    husband of Theophile, I mean of the blessed comes Phoibammon, and of the rents of this (land) under me I
13    make full payment each [year] with all good faith. And the locum-tenens having taken up office
14    of the Antaiopolites formerly, Dios, the most illustrious chancellor, also the son of Theodosios son of Biktor son of Ioulios, proceeded against me on behalf of Theophile and
15    of Dioskoros her most illustrious son, demanded from me, under the magistracy of Athanasios the most eminent, 40 full-weight solidi. And in this manner, on their behalf
16    again, Helladios the most illustrious scriniarius, having become locum-tenens, demanded from me another 8 solidi on their behalf. And not only did they overlook
17    me in these matters, the same Dioskoros and Theophile his mother, having given me nothing of these (sums), but also upon these very (demands)
18    they took from me sixteen circus-horses of mine, 9 of mine and 7 of my mother; and … they forcibly tore away, having prepared … with their documents and the …; and nine cattle, and two beasts of burden, and three chaff-bins of mine
19    filled with hampers of grain and dry hay, and twenty (artabas) of wheat, and one hundred and sixteen jars of wine, and sixty pounds of wool, …
20    and the iron rim of one wheel of my wagon, that is its tire, and the produce of three years
21    that is the wine of the lands under me. Wherefore I adjure you by the immortal head and by the most God-fearing
22    emperors and our universal masters of the diadem, and by your salvation above all things, if it should be possible, to order
23    that I be sufficiently redressed and that my said property and losses be recovered from the said
24    Theophile and Dioskoros; unjustly and on a pretext of theirs and a charge I was made to pay on their behalf to the said Helladios and Dios
25    who held the locum-tenency one after the other at that time, and who dealt with me in hostile fashion. And in return for this benefaction the third part of the (recovered amount) I will provide to your servants by way of a gift for accomplishing it, and the two-thirds that I too may obtain, for the support of my old age
26    [and of my infant] children, your most wretched servants; so that I may continually raise up unceasing intercession and prayer to God, for the preservation
27    and salvation of your benevolence, master.
28    A plea and supplication from Apollos the wretched
29    from Pou[cheos]
30    [village of the Antaiopolite nome].

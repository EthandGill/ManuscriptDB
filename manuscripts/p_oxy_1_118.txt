[TRANSLATION]
1   Saras and Eudaimon
2   to Diogenes their son, greeting.
3   Advised by
4   the most estimable Ammo-
5   nion, because of the uncertainty of the
6   journey, to send for a
7   ferry-boat, we sent
8   you a dispatch so that,
9   if, persuaded by you, they
10   se[n]d (it) while you are [p]res-
11   ent, you may [de]tain what is needed; but if
12   not, you may notify
13   both the strategos a[n]d
14   the peace-officers, for
15   our security,
16   to be put on
17   record. And knowing what
18   the hospitality is like, having got
19   from the priests a little
20   …(?) and some frankincense,
21   [and] buying up some good
22   [stuff,] coming,
23   [brin]g it. We hear
24   [that] two days in
25   [the He]rakleopo-
26   lite (nome) he is, so, with the
27   care that is yours,
28   make haste, having
29   (those) for whose sake you put in to harbor;
30   for there is no profit if the
31   needful things fall
32   short at his arrival.
33   Ammonas and Dioskoros
34   the cooks have gone up to
35   the O[x]yrhynchite (nome), meaning
36   to come out at once. Since, then,
37   they are slow, lest at some point
38   there be need of them, at
39   once drive them out.
40   Farewell, for my good fortune.
41   (2nd hand) Farewell, both for me and for you,
42   good [fortune].
43   To Diogenes.

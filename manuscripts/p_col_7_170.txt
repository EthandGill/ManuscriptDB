[TRANSLATION]
1     To Aurelius Gerontios, praepositus of the 5th pagus,
2     from Aurelius Isidoros son of Ptolemaios, from the village of Karanis. Of the petition which I submitted to my lord, the most distinguished prefect (hegemon) of Herculian Egypt, Valerius Ziper, together with the subscription which I obtained from his greatness, I append the copy below and submit it to you, asking that a copy of these be dispatched to those accused by me, Tomis and Demetrios from the same village, so that they may know what has been commanded and may attend upon your court until the matter between us reaches its conclusion. Farewell. In the consulship of our lords Licinius Augustus for the 5th time and Crispus,
3     the most noble Caesar, for the 1st time, Epeiph 22. And it (the copy) is: To Valerius Ziper, the most distinguished prefect of Herculian
4     Egypt, from Aurelius Isidoros son of Ptolemaios, from the village of Karanis in the Arsinoite nome. During the
5     month Phamenoth, in the consulship of Sabinus and Rufinus, prefect, lord, Kastor and his brother Ammonianos, from the same village, being in need, were supplied by me with thirty-two artabas of grain
6     and eighteen artabas of bean (phaselos), and they made out for me bonds of these on the security (mortgage) of three head of cattle (oxen). And on the same day their father-in-law Tomis made out to me a bond of agreement,
7     through which he undertook the guarantee for the oxen, on condition that, if they did not make the delivery of the pulses to me within the month Pauni, I should clearly hold the ownership of the oxen, in accordance with the bonds.
8     Since, then, Kastor has departed this life and Ammonianos happens to be in flight (anachoresis),
9     and the aforementioned Tomis and his brother Demetrios,
10    who happens to be the husband of the sister of those indebted to me,
11    Atolas, became masters of all the things left by them — of the eight oxen, I mean, and of the building-plots and the rest — providing me with nothing, neither the pulses nor
12    the cattle four-footed, although so much time has elapsed, despising me; on account of this I appeal to your manliness, begging and entreating that, if it seems right to you, you order, through whomever you may approve, that my opponents be compelled either to give back the pulses or to make restitution to me of the three oxen, in accordance with the bonds, and (I undertake) to acknowledge thanks to you. Farewell.
13    Pharmouthi 18. The praepositus of the pagus, having adjudicated between you, will provide you with the assistance fitting
14    upon the debts owed. Kollema 68. I, Aurelius Isidoros, have submitted it. I, Aurelius Serenos, wrote on his behalf, since he is illiterate.

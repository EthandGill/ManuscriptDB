[TRANSLATION]
1     In the (year) after the consulship of our lord Flavius Zeno, the eternal Augustus,
2     for the 3rd time, Pharmouthi 1, of the fourth indiction, in the splendid city of the Lykopolitans.
3     This joint agreement of settlement they make between themselves: on the one
4     [part, Kyr]os, the most God-beloved [bishop] of the ca[tholic] holy church of God of the Lykopolitans,
5     [and Danielios and Areion,] most pious pr[esbyters, the three] true-born [brothers]
6     [from the fath]er, the man of pious memory [Besas; and on the other Theophilos] son of Apollon,
7     [deacon] of the same holy church of God. We acknowledge to one another the following. Greeting. A little earlier the aforementioned [Theophilos, having approached] the great
8     augustalian (court) [by a libellus of accusa]tion, accused the aforewritten
9     Kyros, the most God-fearing bishop, [and Danielios and Areion, the] most pious presbyters,
10    on manifest heads, those also contained in the petition sent by him,
11    and it was agreed that Kyros the most God-beloved bishop, going down
12    to that great tribunal, should set out the pleas he had,
13    if he should not choose to settle the charges before trial; and that Danielios and Areion, his
14    most pious brothers, should plead by rebuttal before Makarios the most eloquent advocate
15    of the Thebaid revenue-office. And these things having been agreed, an agreement has been made
16    between Theophilos the aforewritten [deacon and]
17    Danielios and Areion,
18    the most pious ones, on the sa[me d]ay … i[n] the city of L[ykopo]lis, according to which they agreed
19    with one another to appear within a fixed term before the same most eloquent
20    Makarios and to set out the pleas they had, or else to pay a specified
21    fine according to its provision. And upon these matters, the plain
22    reminders having been submitted by Kyros the most God-fearing bishop from
23    Theophilos, without awaiting a decisive examination, he was prevailed upon
24    to do those things which any who should become arbiters between them should adjudge; and indeed
25    Makarios and Sabinos, the most eloquent, chosen by each party informally,
26    having become arbiters between them and having heard all their pleading, adjudged that Kyros the most pious bishop should pay over to Theophilos
27    sixteen gold pieces, that is 16 gold solidi, for release from every suit and plea
28    and complaint; and that Danielios and Areion should hand over likewise to Theophilos
29    the following listed items thus: one man's short mantle (kolobion-maphorion), one Egyptian
30    hooded cloak (kousoulion), one Egyptian hooded coat (karakallion), one white-rose tunic, two multi-threaded
31    cushions, one shaded coverlet, one honey-colored dalmatic, one bordered blanket,
32    two small coarse curtains, five straight-woven tunic-mantles, one mallow-colored
33    maphorion, one man's plain tunic, two men's linen garments, and linen cloths and towels
34    from Skinepoeus, seven; one small mattress from Skinepoeus; one Damascene shirt;
35    ten face-cloths; two garment-bags; one napkin from Skinepoeus; one facing-cloth
36    from Skinepoeus; one hand-towel; one weaver's garment-bag; three new
37    bordered linen cloths; one bordered woolen (cloth); two other woolen linen
38    cloths; one woman's embroidered face-cloth; and upon these that every
39    occasion of suit be extinguished for Theophilos against the aforewritten three brothers.
40    These things therefore having been carried out in deed and effect, the aforewritten
41    most pious Theophilos, having received from the most God-beloved bishop the aforesaid
42    sixteen gold pieces, that is 16 gold solidi, and from Danielios and Areion the most pious
43    the aforesaid garments and various items, acknowledges that he has no further claim
44    against them, neither any complaint whatever, whether outside courts
45    or in court, small or great, local or foreign, neither against the three
46    jointly nor against one individually, nor concerning a fine, because the aforewritten
47    most God-beloved bishop, for the sake of piety alone and of the reverence
48    belonging to him, chose to acknowledge other moneys to those who from the beginning had held
49    Theophilos' garments and to settle these. And they too acknowledge, the same
50    most God-fearing bishop and Danielios and Areion the most pious brothers, that they have no claim
51    against the same Theophilos, neither in courts nor outside courts,
52    neither small nor great, local or foreign, neither by way of expenses
53    or damages such as might have befallen them through his fault. And they mutually acknowledge, upon formal question, that neither concerning the aforesaid
54    heads nor concerning any other matter whatsoever, whether pertaining
55    to this case or not pertaining, whether thought of or not thought of,
56    whether brought into court or not brought, do they have any
57    claim or complaint or liability against one another up to the aforewritten
58    day, which is Pharmouthi the new-moon (1st) of the present fourth
59    indiction, but that there is extinguished for them against one another every seed of suit and
60    action and liability, both as to person and as to matter; and that they abide by
61    this agreement of settlement and will in no way disturb it
62    or any part of it. They acknowledge that, if anyone should dare to act contrary to it,
63    he shall give to the abiding party, by way of fine, thirty-six
64    gold solidi, that is 36 gold solidi, for the mere attempt,
65    with this settlement being none the less unbroken and unshaken
66    upon the provisions it contains. And upon all these things, having formally questioned
67    one another and having been questioned by one another that these things are thus,
68    they agreed to abide, give, and observe. I, Kyros, the aforewritten bishop, have made this settlement, being persuaded by all that is written herein,
69    and I will abide by it forever and will not bring claim, as aforesaid; and, being questioned, I acknowledged, and, having read and subscribed, I have issued it.
70    I, Daniel son of Besas, the aforewritten presbyter, have made this settlement
71    upon all the heads written herein, and I abide forever and am persuaded
72    of all the terms, and, being questioned, I acknowledged, and, having read and
73    subscribed, I have issued it. I, Areion son of Besas, the aforewritten presbyter, have made
74    this settlement upon all the heads written herein, and I abide
75    forever and am persuaded of all the terms, and, being questioned, I acknowledged
76    and, having read and subscribed, I have issued it. I, Theophilos son of Apollon, deacon,
77    the aforewritten, have made this settlement, being persuaded by all that is written herein,
78    and I will abide by it forever and will not bring claim, as aforesaid; and, being questioned, I acknowledged
79    and issued it. I, Claudius Herakleides son of Alexandros, councillor of the Lykopolitans, being requested, wrote on his behalf,
80    he being present and saying to me that he does not know letters. I, Aurelius Kollouthos son of Besas, from Diadous,
81    bear witness to the settlement, having heard from those who made the settlement. I, Aurelius Dionysios son of Sois, bear witness
82    to the settlement, having heard from those who made the settlement. I, Aurelius Iobinos son of Akorios,
83    bear witness to the settlement, having heard from those who made the settlement. Aurelius Besas.
84    … [I bear witness to] the settlement, having heard [from those who made the settlement] … .
85    Joint settlement of Kyros the most God-fearing bishop and the other brothers and Theophilos.

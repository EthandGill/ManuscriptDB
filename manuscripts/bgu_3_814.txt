[TRANSLATION]
1   To my [sweet]est mother, very many greetings.
2   [Before all] things I pray for your health together with all your (folk).
3   You will do [well], on receiving my
4   letter, to send me two hundred drachmas. When Geminus
5   [came to me], I had nothing but twenty staters,
6   [but now not] one, because I bought a mule-cart and
7   spent [all the] money on it. I wrote these things to you
8   so that you may know. [You will send] me a cloak (abolla) and a hooded mantle (birrus),
9   [...] and a pair of leggings and a pair of
10   coloured garments, and oil, and the little basin(?) as you told me,
11   [and] a heavy cloak(?) and a pair of pillows (cervicaria) and Germanus,
12   [since I have] need of him. For the rest, mother, you will send
13   me my monthly supplies quickly. You told me these things when I came
14   to you: that before you enter your camp(?)
15   I will send you one of your brothers; and you sent me nothing,
16   but you left me thus with nothing,
17   [...] nor anything from anyone else. You did not say that
18   [...] having nothing, neither money nor anything, but you left
19   me thus like a dog; and my father, coming to me,
20   gave me not an obol, not a hooded mantle, not anything; but everyone
21   laughs at me, (saying) that 'his father is a soldier (and) gave him nothing.'
22   He kept saying that 'when I go home I will send you
23   everything' — (but) he sent me nothing. Why? The mother of Valerius
24   sent him a pair of girdles and a jar of oil
25   and a little basket of preserved meat and a money-belt(?) and two hundred drachmas
26   [...]. So I ask you, mother, send (something) to me,
27   do not leave me thus; for I went off (and) have borrowed money
28   from a fellow-soldier and from my optio. And my broth-
29   er Gemellus sent me a letter and trousers (bracae).
30   Know that I am grieved that I did not go off near my brother,
31   and he himself is grieved that I did not go off near him; he
32   sent a letter blaming me, that I went off to another unit.
33   So I write you these things, mother, that you may know. You will do well,
34   on receiving my letter, to send (a reply) to me quickly.
35   Know that my brother Gemellus
36   <sent and> went off to …len. I greet all those in the house,
37   [your folk]; I greet Apollinarios and Valerios and Geminos,
38   [I greet also] all those who love us.
39   (written sideways) [...] you will send to me, if you know that no one [...]
40   [...] my father said to me [...]

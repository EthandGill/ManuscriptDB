[TRANSLATION]
1     Kerdon to Agrippinus, his
2     brother, very many greetings.
3     [Before all] things I pray that you are in good health. I [wish] you to know
4     [th]at your (affairs) bear good fruit, if
5     … your mother in (good) season … to them nine date-palms, [and] the (matters) there are getting on
6     … a[nd] …, that Epagathios went off near the threshing-floor
7     we … since he was about to winnow.
8     Therefore I [wri]te [to you], so that you may know and may send [me] … the (man) with you,
9     and … not nevertheless any longer
10    … knowing, that
11    … upon them as then
12    having heard … from you, that you saw
13    my (affairs); for no longer am I about to be very proud,
14    and I rejoice that you did these things for me,
15    I myself being regretful about nothing.
16    For having a clean soul (I am) of nothing … of the slanderers … [a]nd if you are about to do anything concerning the inset(?); for no one
17    … there, except us
18    al[one]; for the farmers obstruct us [and] mean to bring suit against us; know then that the
19    days are against us. Therefore I write to you, that you may know; and know
20    that we … not … to beg off the collectors; for these things were registered
21    in addition as of a weaver's (account), 39 drachmas.
22    A[nd otherw]ise then you will write to me concerning the things which
23    seem good to you [to write], but more necessarily
24    concerning the inset(?). Do not neglect … again the grain (affairs), when the half
25    of them … lest, on account of the weaver
26    of yours saying that I wrote for my (grain) to be brought in. Greet
27    all your (people), and perhaps you are able
28    to revive … the watering-place, but if
29    not, it dries up. I pray for your good health.

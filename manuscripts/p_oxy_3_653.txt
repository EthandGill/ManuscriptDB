[TRANSLATION]
1     apomoira (tax)
2     eparourion (land-surtax)
3     freight (naulon)
4     of cargoes
5     libation (sponde)
6     From the memoranda of Lucius Volusius Ma[eci]anus, [year 2] … of [Anto]ninus Cae[sa]r [the] l[or]d … 1, … of …enes son of Julius V[o]ltimus [in] Paraitonion, who also answered the summons, in the presence of Sempr[oni]us Orestinus son of Sempronius [T]arantinus
7     of the col[on]y … the [c]ase was to be conducted here. Isidor[us the rh]etor, on behalf of Sempr[onius Orestinus, having an]swered before Sempronius Honoratus the tribune, (said) that the matter had been chosen (taken up) and that Voltimus had been condemned.
8     (Document) of Julius Fidus, secretary.
9     Orestinus saying that he had used lawful (means), Maecianus said: the la[wful things] … of [g]ain … is wont to come about. It is said to you: concerning the loan, it stands as the tribune decided … [but the intere]st is far more than the loan; take back the loan and give back the securities. Isidorus said: … we have carried off all this man's lands; this is justly said against us. And certain persons having petitioned [and stat]ing that (the sums) were owed [also to the] colony and that there were creditors of Fi[d]us, Honoratus ordered them to be sold. Maecianu[s sai]d: take the [loan], and do not concern yourself about the rest. Isidorus said: let him also pay back the interest. Maecianus said: [you] reap these (fruits). Isidorus saying that he had not reaped (them), Maecianus said: you took possession. Whether, then, the opponents are present or not present, they shall receive a judge who, following the decision of Honoratus, will examine the Kanopitis (land) so that neither the credito[r] [be di]minished nor the debtor, and neither be in profit. And Orestinus saying again that the property was not in his hands, Maec[i]anus said: willing or unwilling you shall restore it to him; and if you do not do this, you shall not only be condemned but also flogged … only if
10    but if certain others reckon the securities to be liable to themselves, they shall see to it … to take a [ju]dge. Crepinus saying "whomever you may give," Maecianus said: the tribune, whom we deem [it ri]ght to summon.

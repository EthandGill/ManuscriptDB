1     … the same gold of the most magnificent Sarapammon, and Biktor the notary who is also deputy. For I myself neither received (it), but the assistant received the said gold and gave (it) to his headman
2     [together also] with Biktor the deputy, amounting to one and a half pounds of gold. But what else the headman himself took, I do not know. And this same gold he received from his headman,
3     the same most glorious Sarapammon. The illustrious count of the soldiers said: Sarapammon the most magnificent, having heard the statement of Menas saying all that has been said to those standing by,
4     [namely] that it was gold and was demanded from Zacharias and was paid to him, what has he to say about this? Sarapammon, mentioned above, illustrious, said: I learned that certain people from the village of Aphrodite are forming a faction
5     … they wish to make the village (so) that again they may pursue (it) with murders, and on this account, having made the faction, they were charged one pound of gold for the magistrates' fund.
6     The illustrious count of the soldiers said: Talis will press a charge against Sarapammon the most magnificent on account of the extortion of Zacharias. But what also concerning the accusations has Menas to say? Flavius
7     Menas, soldier, said: I killed no one, and I will prove (it). But what … Menas? The same presbyter remained ailing in the church, I (was) in Antaiou (city), and there came out
8     on the neck of the presbyter himself an abscess, and from it he was dying; for concerning Herakleios I was not even there nor do I know (anything). The illustrious count of the soldiers said: If those standing by wish
9     to join in pressing the accusations by written charge … let them speak the law. Flavius, excusing Theodoros, said: In the last days of the past month Mesore,
10    on the waning seventh of the indiction, Menas the ill-disposed, having dragged out my brother Biktor who was also presbyter, killed (him), throwing a mangonel timber onto his left
11    … hand, and inflicting many blows upon his stomach, from the fifth hour until evening of the same day; and I invoke against him the law that is fitting for me
12    against murderers. If I do not prove that he killed my brother, I die for this. He did not survive the blows inflicted on him, but lived only
13    ten or eleven days. Mariam, mentioned above, excusing Theodoros, said: The headmen of Aphrodite my village, with others assisting her, those who arrested my husband
14    Herakleios, put (him) in the prison of my village Aphrodite, and taking wine into the same prison they drank with him, and when evening came, cutting down
15    Herakleios my husband, they slew (him) with swords, and after this they delivered his remains to fire. These things they did against him on the eighth of the month Phaophi of the past
16    sixth indiction, at sunset. And when asked why ever they killed my husband, they said that Sarapammon the most glorious and Menas wrote to us to slay him, and
17    I invoke against them the law that is fitting for me concerning the death of my wretched husband. And when Herakleios my wretched husband was murdered and his remains delivered to fire, after it was burned, again
18    they poured the same remains with water, and put his bones into a small basket, and I do not know where they have placed them. I beseech therefore that these be given back to me, that I may find (means) to bury them. For concerning this very matter I came
19    to the most glorious Sarapammon, and he agreed to arrange that these be given back to me, and they were not given to me.

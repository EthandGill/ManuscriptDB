[TRANSLATION]
1     … having had good fortune … the helmsman, and of the longed-for, if of storm late at last,
2     having met with calm; for the grievous things of those who existed before or who have ended
3     … toward pleasure … … … … the sycophant then …
4     to himself, having leisure from his nature, urges either toward better
5     to be reshaped
6     a disposition, or to yield to these unbribable times.
7     But the greedy man will change his insatiable nature into a self-sufficient choice;
8     he has become teacher to himself, being cautious even before the trial
9     of the danger. And the neighboring barbarians, by bloodless report
10    fearfully and even before deeds laying aside their quiet, a pretext
11    of safety supposing it, welcome this, prudently
12    having deliberated, and granting to themselves, not to us, the greatest things.
13    Defend us therefore even in these things in all respects, most humane ones;
14    driving from the land those called buccellarii,
15    those who hire themselves out for unlawful battle-array, and
16    those who reckon another's toil their own gain; and the household slave
17    bearing arms, not allowing him to speak freely, increasing rather than lessening
18    the wickedness of his master; and ordering the private man as a private man
19    to live henceforth and not to use iron against his own kinsmen.
20    For these things even the imperial fear forbids,
21    having fortified the fear with laws and having established you as
22    exact guardians of the laws.
23    Before all things I do much obeisance and greet your acknowledgment before …
24    the most noble soldier who ought to be stretched out …
25    and that no one be dragged from the divine effigies by force, which is dear to God the Savior,
26    and a care to the Fortune ruling over all, and zealously pursued by you the lawful ones.

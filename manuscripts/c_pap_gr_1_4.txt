[TRANSLATION]
1     To Protarchos.
2     From Sillis son of [Pto]lemaios Philometoreios, and
3     from his mother Philotera daughter of
4     Theodor[os, a Per]si[a]n, with her guardian her husband P[tolemaios] son of Sillis Philometoreios. C[oncerning the matters in dis]pute, Philotera agrees
5     that she will provide her own slave-woman Zosime[s] for the period of two years from Pharmouthi [of the pre]sent 17th year of Caesar, nursing and su[ckl]ing at her, Philotera's, side,
6     the irrecoverable(?) slave-child which Skillis has given out to her,
7     his own female slave-child, still at the breast,
8     whose name is Agalmation, at the established wage
9     both for the milk and for the nursing per each month, together with oil and bread, of 12 drachmas of silver;
10    and the total capital of the nursing-wages of the two years, the 288 drachmas of silver, forthwith
11    Philotera has received from Sillis
12    by ha[nd] from the house. And if it should happen that the said
13    child Agalmation should suffer something human (i.e. die)
14    within the two years, it is obligatory for Philotera, taking up another child, to furnish the slave-woman as nurse and to present (her), all (the wage) [for] whatever (child) she may take up, the whole two years
15    being completed, taking nothing at all in addition, because she has undertaken the nursing as an undying (obligation); and from
16    now on she is to furnish the slave-woman, who exercises the fitting care both of herself and of the child, not spoiling the milk,
17    nor lying with a man, nor becoming pregnant,
18    nor giving suck to another child; and the things which
19    she may receive or be entrusted of his she will keep safe and restore when they are demanded, or pay the value of each, except for evident loss, which, when also it has been made manifest, let her be released; and that she shall not abandon the
20    nursing within the time. And if she transgresses anything of these things, she is to pay what she has received
21    of the nursing-wages as capital together with one-half in addition
22    and the damages and expenses and another
23    penalty of 500 drachmas of silver and the fixed fine, the right of execution belonging to Sillis both from herself, Philotera, and from
24    all her property as if by legal
25    judgment, void being any pledges which she may bring forward, of all protection; and there is to remain valid and secure
26    the agreement which Philotera has reported to Sillis concerning the slave-
27    foster-child. We make this request.
28    Year 17 of Caesar, Pharmouthi 4.

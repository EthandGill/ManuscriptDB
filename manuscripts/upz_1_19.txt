[TRANSLATION]
1     To king Ptolemy and queen [Cleop]atra his sister, to the gods
2     Philometor: greetings. Thais [and] Taus, twins, serving in the
3     great Serapeum toward Memphis, making libations to Osirapis on behalf of both
4     you and your children. Suffering many wrongs from Nephóris
5     and Pachrates her son, we have made appeal to you, in order that we might obtain justice.
6     For the aforementioned Nephóris, living with a certain Philip of those from Memphis
7     …
8     was working, [seeming] that the aforementioned Philip would restore him in the
9     uncertain unmixed times. Sitting down by the door of his house
10    which was toward the river in the Egyptian market-place, our father
11    having gone out and having thought and having swum down into the river and barely
12    having been saved on a certain island and having been taken up into a nearby
13    boat and thence no longer daring to come down, but having separated
14    into the Herakleopolite [region, because] we were not with him [he] changed life.
15    And his brothers having sailed up and having brought him to the necropolis around Memphis, until now [Nephóris] has not dared
16    to bury him. And his property having been taken up into the royal [treasury,]
17    [Nephóris ransomed], having sold half of a house which was common to us and her
18    for bronze talents seven, and brought away property worth bronze talents sixty and rents [it] out monthly
19    bronze drachmae two-hundred, and from these shares nothing to us. Not satisfied with this, [she] expelled us so that
20    [we] were in danger of being destroyed by famine. We having thought of a certain Ptolemy
21    of those in custody in the great Sarapeion, our father's
22    friend, and going to him we were maintained. Upon the mourning of Apis coming,
23    they bring us to mourn for the god. Our mother's
24    friends persuading us to take in her son to serve us,
25    and this having come to pass, having watched us after some time and having taken [the] thing written
26    [to] us [as a] symbol by those at the offices,
27    to the one over the olive-measure, one measure of olive-oil which was due to us
28    to be given by you,
29    and this one he secretly brought, and what copper [coins] we once had
30    and other things, having robbed us, he took off to his mother, so that we
31    might not even have necessities. Therefore we ask you to send forth the petition to
32    Dionysius, one of the friends and general, so that he might write to Apollonios the supervisor
33    and Dorion the counter-clerk: neither the olive-oil becoming to us and castor-oil nor any other nothing
34    belonging to her from ours, but compel her, if the paternal property of ours
35    she holds against law, to return [it], so that we might be helped through you.
36    Farewell.
